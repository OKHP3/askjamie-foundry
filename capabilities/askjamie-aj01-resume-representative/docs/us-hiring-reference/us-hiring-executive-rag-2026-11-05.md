## E01. The central finding

The durable requirement is a hiring system that connects actual work to relevant evidence, consistent evaluation, an explainable offer, and accountable human decisions. Digital tools can support that system without establishing its validity. OPM's job-analysis guidance and current selection research support careful definition and evaluation of the work rather than reliance on tool labels (OPM n.d.-a; Sackett et al. 2023).

The full reference treats AI as secondary. The main subjects are requirements, sourcing, resume review, matching, interviews, assessments, compensation, expectations, and HR operations. It distinguishes observations, analysis, operating proposals, and future scenarios. That classification should remain visible in any answer generated from the file. Continue at USH-01 to USH-03.

## E02. Historical trajectory and current interval

**2010–2014:** the national unemployment rate averaged 9.6 percent in 2010. The period's digital transition is documented from the applicant side: a 2015 Pew survey found that 79 percent of recent job seekers used online resources in their most recent search. Neither figure measures ATS rejection or recruiter behavior directly (BLS 2011; Smith 2015).

**2015–2019:** unemployment averaged 5.3 percent in 2015, while the seasonally adjusted monthly rate reached 3.5 percent in September 2019. These are different temporal measures that support a broad tightening interpretation, not a universal candidate-market label for every occupation (BLS 2016, 2019a). The full report also examines digital recruitment themes and jurisdiction-specific salary-history restrictions.

**2020–2024:** the initial April 2020 employment release reported 14.7 percent unemployment. Census later reported that the share primarily working from home rose from 5.7 percent in 2019 to 17.9 percent in 2021. The remote-work measure is not the share with any hybrid arrangement or the percentage of video interviews (BLS 2020; Census Bureau 2022).

**2025–November 5, 2026:** this is an incomplete current interval, not an observed five-year period. The national unemployment observation specifically verified for this edition is September 2026, at 4.2 percent. No November rate is inferred (BLS 2026). Continue at USH-04 to USH-07.

## E03. Resume screening and candidate matching

Parsing extracts information; screening applies criteria; ranking orders results; a hiring decision selects an action. Those functions should not be collapsed into one supposed ATS score. Greenhouse documents that an unsuccessful parse can leave a resume attached while fields require manual entry. A parse failure is therefore not evidence of automatic rejection across all employers (Greenhouse 2026).

The recommended review design separates legitimate eligibility requirements, supported evidence, unresolved questions, and verification. A job title does not establish scope. A technology mention does not prove mastery. A team achievement does not establish sole ownership. The employer should assess claims rather than infer capability from document polish.

Transferable skills are hypotheses to examine. A candidate can demonstrate relevant concepts while still needing product-specific training. Use requirement-level evidence rather than unsupported success probabilities. A phrase such as “demonstrated customer diagnosis; workflow proficiency not assessed” is more useful than an unexplained matching percentage. These are operating recommendations, not validated scoring instruments. Continue at USH-08 to USH-11.

## E04. Interviews and assessment

Structured interviews use planned job-relevant questions and consistent evaluation. Research reassessing selection validity supports their usefulness while emphasizing that method averages do not guarantee local results (OPM n.d.-b; Sackett et al. 2022). A label alone is insufficient: questions, scoring anchors, interviewer training, and administration matter.

The proposed process asks interviewers to record evidence and initial ratings before a panel debrief. Disagreements should be resolved against the criteria, not by seniority or personal affinity. Add another assessment only when it could resolve a consequential unanswered question.

Work samples should resemble relevant tasks and match the capability expected at entry. OPM identifies both their strengths and their costs and cautions against testing as an entry requirement a competency that is meant to be taught after selection (OPM n.d.-c). State time expectations, permitted tools, evaluation criteria, accessibility options, and the use of candidate data. Do not require confidential work samples or turn testing into undisclosed commercial labor. Continue at USH-12, USH-13, and USH-15.

## E05. Skills-first hiring requires operational change

Removing a degree from a posting is not evidence that hiring outcomes changed. A 2024 observational study found a gap between degree-removal announcements and actual hiring changes in its selected large-employer sample (Sigelman, Fuller, and Martin 2024).

The proposed skills-first conversion connects revised requirements, reviewer training, assessments, development support, and actual outcomes. Legally required credentials remain distinct from unnecessary proxies. A trainable candidate needs a real training plan, not a promise that disappears when work becomes urgent. Continue at USH-14 and USH-27.

## E06. Salary setting and expectations

Market pricing begins with job scope, level, location, and pay definitions, not a title lookup. OEWS occupational wages are not individualized offers or total rewards; its definitions and population boundaries must be respected. The Employment Cost Index measures employer labor-cost changes and is not a universal raise recommendation (BLS n.d.-a, n.d.-b).

A proposed compensation record identifies the job match, source dates, pay components, internal comparisons, range placement, and reason for any exception. Compa-ratio and range penetration describe placement in a chosen structure; they do not prove the structure is valid. The full report includes explicitly synthetic arithmetic examples.

Work-arrangement expectations should be equally concrete. “Hybrid” needs an operational definition. Travel, schedule, location, assessment burden, and development support should be communicated accurately. Candidate satisfaction, offer acceptance, retention, and performance are different outcomes and should not be treated as interchangeable evidence. Continue at USH-17 to USH-20.

## E07. Legal and accessibility boundaries

This companion is not a compliance checklist. Federal selection guidance, consumer-report requirements, disability accommodation, wage-discussion rights, and state or local obligations have distinct coverage and tests. The employer's responsibilities are not transferred wholesale to a vendor (EEOC 2007; FTC and EEOC 2014; DOJ 2022).

Three currentness warnings are particularly important. Federal contractor materials predating the January 2025 rescission of Executive Order 11246 require rechecking. Illinois Public Act 103-0804 took effect January 1, 2026, but this edition does not certify implementing-rule status. Colorado's May 2026 law replaced the earlier AI framework, with specified new requirements beginning January 1, 2027. Future operation is not current nationwide law (DOL 2025; Illinois General Assembly 2024; Colorado General Assembly 2026).

New York City's requirements apply to covered automated employment decision tools under defined conditions, not every use of AI. A required bias audit does not automatically establish validity, accessibility, privacy compliance, or compliance elsewhere (NYC DCWP n.d.). Consult the full legal section and the actual governing sources before operational use. Continue at USH-16, USH-18, and USH-21.

## E08. AI and the HR operating model

The recommended distinction is administrative assistance versus consequential assessment or decision support. Each use needs an owner, intended purpose, suitable evidence, access controls, version tracking, and an exception process. Review must be meaningful: a person needs enough information and authority to challenge the tool, not merely approve its output.

SHRM's 2025 panel reported AI use in HR tasks at 43 percent. Its scope is broader than hiring, its data are unweighted, and the figure is a 2025 milestone rather than a current census (SHRM 2025). Adoption does not establish effectiveness.

The proposed HR capability model includes requirement design, candidate communication, assessment literacy, compensation analysis, accessibility, privacy, vendor assurance, and measurement. It does not predict a specific national recruiter headcount or require a new job title in every organization. Continue at USH-22 to USH-24.

## E09. Two future projection points

**2031:** the baseline scenario is more connected evidence and workflow management, not universal autonomous hiring. An accelerated scenario depends on better assessment, development capacity, and interoperable evidence. A constrained scenario includes fragmented rules, weak validation, security failures, and candidate distrust. Watch actual hiring and outcomes rather than announcements.

**2036:** plausible paths include more continuous links between recruitment, internal mobility, learning, and staffing, or several deliberately different hiring models by role. Risks include concentrated personal data, unverifiable credentials, excessive surveillance, and automated amplification of historical disadvantage. More technology is not inherently better governance.

These are authored scenarios without assigned probabilities. They are not empirical findings and are not promises that any law, credential system, work arrangement, or profession will develop in a particular way. Continue at USH-25 and USH-26.
