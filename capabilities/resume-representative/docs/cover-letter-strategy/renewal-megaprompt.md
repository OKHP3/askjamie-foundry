### 3. Scope contract

The subject is evidence-led cover-letter creation: employer and role research; applicant evidence; truthful persuasion; requirement mapping; letter structure and voice; confidentiality; worked examples; retrieval design; evaluation; and renewal.

Include platform facts only where necessary to explain reference use, tool boundaries, or portability. Exclude unrelated model histories, compensation guides, broad hiring-market forecasts, and private candidate dossiers unless the user explicitly expands scope. Preserve prior structure only where it serves this contract. A prior edition is a baseline to audit, not a canon that cannot be corrected.

Do not claim to know a manager's private motives or vulnerabilities. Do not fabricate achievements, credentials, metrics, sources, or current company problems. Do not equate adjacent experience with direct experience. Do not promise ATS success, interview invitations, or hiring outcomes.

### 4. Establish coverage and a source ledger

Read the supplied prior edition and available supporting material. Record exactly which files, sections, history windows, and attachments were inspected. Record inaccessible originals and ambiguous versions. A title, filename, memory summary, or old assistant assertion is not proof of source access. A summary is not a lossless transcript.

Build a ledger with: source ID, author or organization, title, publication date when established, observation date, URL or authorized private locator, relevant claims, access status, and reuse/privacy limits. Separate public methodological references from private applicant facts. Do not publish private locators, transcripts, career records, or employer-confidential details.

Classify consequential claims as confirmed, inferred, proposed, or unknown. Clarify whether confirmed means candidate-supplied, document-inspected, or independently corroborated. Resolve conflicts by inspecting underlying evidence, not automatically by choosing the newest, longest, or most flattering source.

### 5. Audit the previous edition before renewal

Create a compact acceptance map covering scope, substantive depth, evidence, citation traceability, examples, privacy, retrieval structure, metadata, rendering, and portability. Identify content to retain, revise, add, or remove, with reasons.

Check specifically for these historical failure modes: off-topic GPT history; unverifiable citation markers; automatic claims of file access; universal ATS claims; unsupported psychological profiling; false page counts; backdated creation dates; changing copyright merely because the edition year changed; waiving literal headers and footers; and treating a reference prompt as a system-level override.

Preserve meaningful prior decisions and corrections in a change log. Do not silently launder an old error into a newly sourced statement. Do not republish unsafe or misleading instructions as active guidance merely for continuity; summarize their rejection with enough provenance to explain the correction.

### 7. Write the complete knowledge reference

Produce substantial, coherent chapters on the scope topics. Include at least two complete, explicitly fictional worked examples with their input facts, requirement mappings, finished letters, and review commentary. Keep all real candidate data private unless the user has authorized the specific audience and wording.

Explain practical decisions: what to do when evidence is missing, sources conflict, a qualification is absent, a company signal is ambiguous, a metric lacks a denominator, a result belongs to a team, or an example is confidential. Show repairs to weak or overstated wording. Distinguish recommended practices from empirical claims.

Use concise paragraphs and descriptive hierarchical headings. Preserve stable semantic IDs where their meaning survives. Keep qualifications and citations adjacent to the claims they limit. Include a glossary and reusable data contracts. Avoid long stretches of generic motivational prose and gratuitous named-method lists.

Use constitutional rules for factual integrity, privacy, and action boundaries. Use SCAMPER only to vary presentation without changing facts. Use optimizer-style comparison with a fixed evidence ledger and explicit rubric. Provide an audit-friendly rationale and evidence summary, not hidden chain-of-thought. Distinguish actual tool use from simulated roles and author review from independent evaluation.

### 8. Make retrieval testable
