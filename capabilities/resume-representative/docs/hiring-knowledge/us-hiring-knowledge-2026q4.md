# U.S. Hiring Documents and Selection Processes

## 01. Executive overview

Section-ID: HIR-001. Tags: hiring-system; candidate-evidence; executive-overview. Evidence type: synthesis and recommendations. Review date: 2026-11-05.

The enduring function of hiring paperwork is to turn a proposed employment relationship into evidence that can be evaluated, compared, authorized, and retained. The medium has changed considerably since 2000. A mailed résumé, an uploaded résumé, a structured application record, an interview scorecard, and a conditional-offer checklist perform different parts of that function. None should be treated as a complete representation of the person or as a substitute for a defensible selection decision.

The central operating recommendation of this resource is to manage hiring as a sequence of evidence decisions rather than as a pile of documents. Define what a job requires, identify what information demonstrates those requirements, collect it at an appropriate stage, evaluate it consistently, and preserve the decision trail without collecting unnecessary personal information. Candidates benefit from the corresponding discipline: maintain accurate career evidence, select relevant examples, follow the actual application instructions, and distinguish persuasive presentation from unsupported claims.

The historical record supports a transition toward digital and mobile applications, but not a single national timeline applicable to every employer. Pew's 2015 survey found that 79 percent of Americans who had looked for work during the preceding two years used online resources in their most recent search. That is a dated survey finding about job seekers, not a 2026 measure of applicant tracking system adoption. It helps explain why digital accessibility became consequential without proving that all hiring became automated. (Smith 2015)

Technology is a secondary theme in this document. An applicant tracking system can store documents, extract fields, route applications, support searches, and sometimes apply configured screening or scoring. These functions must not be collapsed into a mythical single algorithm. Greenhouse's documentation, for example, describes unsuccessful parsing as a failure to populate candidate fields that can require manual entry. It does not say that every failed parse automatically rejects the applicant. (Greenhouse 2026)

Current operational guidance must also be date-sensitive. Federal résumé guidance changed in 2025; federal contractor regulations changed in 2026; and some automated-decision requirements are enacted but not yet effective. A knowledge file that preserves old advice without legal status labels can be more misleading than a short document that states its limits. This edition therefore separates historical developments, verified current examples, recommended practices, and forecasts. (OPM 2025; DOL 2026; Colorado Attorney General 2026)

The future outlook is conditional rather than deterministic. More verifiable evidence, better-structured assessments, clearer notices, and lower-friction applications are credible directions. Universal disappearance of résumés, universal AI interviewing, and blockchain-based employment records are not established outcomes. The organization should invest first in evidence quality and process control, then assess technology against those needs.

## 02. Scope, evidence method, and interpretation rules

This is a selected-source professional synthesis, not a systematic literature review, an exhaustive fifty-state legal survey, or a substitute for counsel. The forward-looking window is 2027 through 2035. A source published in 2025 can describe observations from 2023; publication year, observation period, and legal effective date are kept conceptually separate.

The source hierarchy favors statutes and official regulator guidance for legal propositions, original research for empirical claims, and vendor documentation for the operation of that vendor's product. University career resources support document-purpose and writing guidance. A vendor description is not proof of industry prevalence, scientific validity, or employer compliance. An agency guidance page is not itself a newly enacted law. An abstract or institutional summary supports only the proposition visible there, not every result in an unread full paper.

Their inclusion does not certify that every applicable exception, local amendment, court decision, or industry-specific obligation has been collected. ## 03. Vocabulary and the hiring evidence lifecycle

Section-ID: HIR-003. Tags: terminology; curriculum-vitae; hiring-stages; artifact-taxonomy. Evidence type: definitions and proposed operating model.

The correct term is curriculum vitae, conventionally abbreviated CV. In U.S. academic and research hiring, a CV generally provides a fuller record of education, research, publications, teaching, and related scholarly work. A business résumé is normally a selected account of relevant qualifications and experience. A cover letter explains interest and fit in narrative form. These distinctions are useful, but the employer's announcement controls the requested document and content. (Harvard MCS n.d.)

In this operating model, a prospect is a person identified through sourcing or networking who has not necessarily applied. An applicant has supplied information for an opening. A candidate is someone being considered within a selection process, although organizations use that word differently. A finalist has reached a later evaluation stage. A conditional offeree has received an offer subject to stated conditions. A new hire has entered the employment and onboarding process. These working definitions do not replace any statutory definition of applicant, employee, or covered person.

A requisition authorizes a hiring need. A job analysis identifies tasks and competencies. A job description describes the role; a public job posting communicates the opportunity and applicable disclosures. An application is a structured submission to a particular opening. A résumé is one evidence artifact within that submission. An assessment or interview produces additional evidence. A scorecard records evaluation against criteria. A disposition record states what happened to the application. An offer letter states proposed terms, while subsequent verification and onboarding records serve different functions.

The recommended lifecycle contains six decision boundaries: authorize the role; invite and receive applications; determine basic eligibility; assess capability; decide and condition an offer; complete lawful verification and onboarding. Not every employer uses six named stages, and stages can overlap operationally. The value of the model is to make information timing explicit. A payroll tax form should not become a test of candidate suitability, and a confidential accommodation record should not be circulated as an interviewer's résumé attachment.

“Pre-candidate paperwork” is used here as the user's broad label for pre-application and early-selection materials. It is not a uniform legal category. The report distinguishes early forms from conditional-offer and post-acceptance paperwork to avoid implying that an employer should request every document from every applicant. This distinction matters more than whether a form is delivered on paper, in a portal, through a vendor, or by electronic signature.

## 04. Historical development: 2000–2009

Section-ID: HIR-004. Tags: hiring-history; internet-applications; electronic-signatures; 2000s. Evidence type: historical synthesis.

The early 2000s should be understood as a period of overlapping channels rather than an overnight replacement of paper. Employer websites, email, online job search, telephone contact, referrals, career fairs, and in-person applications could coexist. The defensible historical conclusion is that online information and electronic submissions became increasingly consequential, not that a particular year ended paper hiring nationwide. An employer's size, occupation mix, labor market, and technical capacity affected its process.

Research on internet job search illustrates why a simple progress narrative is inadequate. Kuhn and Mansour examined job search during 2005–2008 and reported a different association between internet use and reemployment from earlier findings. Their study concerns a defined population and period; it does not prove that creating more online applications always causes faster hiring. The practical lesson is to distinguish channel adoption from evidence that a channel improves outcomes for a particular applicant. (Kuhn and Mansour 2014)

The Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act supplied an important legal foundation for electronic transactions. Its central rule prevents covered records and signatures from being denied legal effect solely because they are electronic. That does not waive substantive disclosure, consent, record-access, or other legal requirements. An electronic checkbox can capture an acknowledgment, but the meaning and sufficiency of that acknowledgment depend on what was presented and the governing law. (U.S. Congress 2000)

Digital applications made it possible to collect standardized fields separately from a candidate's résumé. From an information-design perspective, this created a distinction that remains important: the uploaded document is a presentation, while structured fields are data used by a workflow. A candidate could supply a strong narrative and still leave a required eligibility question unanswered. Conversely, a complete form could provide too little evidence of performance. Effective applications need both accurate structured responses and relevant supporting material.

Fairness concerns did not begin with AI. Bertrand and Mullainathan's correspondence experiment sent fictitious résumés responding to Boston and Chicago newspaper advertisements and documented different callback rates associated with the names used. Its findings demonstrate discrimination in that experimental setting; they should not be converted into an estimate for every employer or the current national market. The broader analytical implication is that digitizing a process does not, by itself, remove discrimination already present in judgment and opportunity allocation. (Bertrand and Mullainathan 2004)

The EEOC's 2007 selection guidance explicitly addressed employment tests and selection procedures in a context of increasing applications and screening. That source is evidence that employers were already balancing efficiency with nondiscrimination well before generative AI. The recurring management problem was not simply whether to automate, but whether the criteria and methods used to screen people were appropriate for the work. (EEOC 2007)

## 05. Historical development: 2010–2019

Section-ID: HIR-005. Tags: hiring-history; mobile-applications; digital-divide; 2010s. Evidence type: historical synthesis and design implications.

By the middle of the 2010s, online job search had become prominent enough to make digital access a material part of opportunity. Pew's 2015 research also found that 28 percent of American adults had used a smartphone in a job search; among those smartphone job seekers, about half had completed an application on the device. These are different denominators from the 79 percent statistic for recent job seekers. Combining them into a single “mobile application rate” would be incorrect. (Smith 2015)

For application design, the implication is straightforward: a process that works only on a large screen or assumes uninterrupted broadband can impose a barrier unrelated to job capability. This is an original design inference, not a claim that mobile compatibility guarantees better hires. Employers should test form completion, file attachment, saving, error recovery, and accommodation access on the actual devices candidates may use. A portal's existence does not establish its usability.

Professional profiles and portfolios also complicated the boundary of the application. Candidates could present a selected résumé, a fuller professional profile, and external examples of work. This report does not assign a universal adoption percentage to those artifacts. Instead, it recommends treating them as distinct evidence views. A profile can supply breadth, a résumé can emphasize the target role, and a work sample can demonstrate a capability. Differences in detail are not automatically contradictions; incompatible dates, unearned titles, or false claims are.

The decade's process lesson is that scale and standardization can diverge. Receiving more applications is not the same as defining better criteria. A portal can collect hundreds of applications while a manager still makes unstructured decisions. A structured interview, by contrast, connects questions and evaluation to job competencies. The strength of structure comes from the procedure, not from whether the interview occurs in a meeting room or through a digital tool. (OPM n.d.a)

A reliable historical narrative should resist declaring that cover letters “died,” that every résumé became two pages, or that all employers adopted ranking algorithms. Such claims require representative evidence across occupations and employer types. This edition treats the 2010s as a period in which digital channels and structured records increasingly shaped the candidate experience, while recognizing that local practices remained heterogeneous.

## 06. Historical development: 2020–2026

Section-ID: HIR-006. Tags: hiring-history; pandemic; hybrid-work; legal-refresh; 2020s. Evidence type: documented findings and synthesis.

The pandemic period changed the feasibility and expectations of remote interaction. One measurable indicator is work location, not hiring format: Census Bureau analysis reports that the share of workers usually working from home was 5.7 percent in 2019, 17.9 percent in 2021, and 13.8 percent in 2023. These American Community Survey estimates should not be presented as the percentage of jobs that can be remote, the percentage of interviews held online, or a current 2026 rate. (Burrows, Burd, and Ismail 2025)

The hiring implication is an inference: where employers and candidates can interact remotely, interview logistics and assessment delivery can be decoupled from travel. That does not eliminate the need to specify the eventual work arrangement. A fully remote interview may recruit for an on-site job. A hybrid position may require residence in a particular state or regular attendance at a particular office. Application and offer records should make those conditions explicit rather than relying on an ambiguous “remote-friendly” label.

Skills-based hiring became an important policy and management theme, but announcements are not equivalent to changes in actual hiring. The Burning Glass Institute and Harvard Business School research describes a gap between removing degree requirements and changing who is hired. This matters for both candidates and employers: the deletion of a degree line does not prove that an assessment has become more accessible or that selection decisions now emphasize demonstrated skills. (Sigelman, Fuller, and Martin 2024)

The period also illustrates why hiring knowledge cannot be updated by changing the year in a title. OPM guidance introduced a two-page résumé limit for USAJOBS beginning September 27, 2025. A long federal résumé recommendation copied from an older guide can therefore be wrong for a current announcement. The relevant source and the specific announcement must be checked, including required qualification detail and supporting documents. (OPM 2025)

Federal contractor requirements require similarly careful temporal treatment. The Department of Labor published a final rule rescinding regulations implementing Executive Order 11246, effective October 26, 2026. This edition does not treat the older EO11246 framework or its associated internet-applicant discussion as a universal current compliance checklist. Other applicable civil-rights, disability, veteran, recordkeeping, and contractual obligations require separate analysis; one rescission is not repeal of all employment law. (DOL 2026)

The historical conclusion is not “AI replaced hiring.” It is that hiring became a more complex combination of electronic records, remote interaction, skills evidence, administrative controls, and rapidly changing legal requirements. Generative tools add new opportunities and risks, but the quality of the process still depends on job relevance, truthfulness, accessibility, and accountable decisions.

## 07. Start with job analysis and a defensible requisition

Section-ID: HIR-007. Tags: requisition; job-analysis; competency-model; employer-controls. Evidence type: recommended operating practice.

A strong hiring process begins before anyone writes a résumé or submits an application. The employer should define the work to be performed, expected outcomes, essential functions, decision authority, work conditions, and qualifications needed at entry. OPM's hiring guidance places confirmation of job analysis and assessment strategy in the hiring process. That provides a sound starting principle: decide what to assess before selecting an assessment because a vendor offers it. (OPM n.d.c)

The recommended requisition record contains a unique opening identifier, business owner, budget authority, job family and level, location and work arrangement, employment type, expected compensation basis, essential functions, and decision criteria. It should distinguish a true opening from a general talent-pipeline invitation. A record of approval is different from permission to publish, and a published posting is different from authorization to make an offer. Keeping these states distinct reduces avoidable confusion.

Separate requirements into three groups. First are genuine eligibility constraints, such as a legally necessary license or an unavoidable work-location condition. Second are capabilities expected on entry, which need a defined evidence method. Third are preferences or capabilities that can be learned after hiring. Do not convert every preference into a knockout criterion. When experience is used as a proxy, explain why it matters and whether equivalent evidence is acceptable.

Avoid writing a job description by simply accumulating every technology, credential, and responsibility mentioned by stakeholders. That creates a specification for an imaginary employee rather than an executable selection process. Ask which outcomes a successful employee must deliver, which capabilities are necessary to deliver them, and which are shared with the team. A requirement that cannot be connected to the work should be challenged before it becomes an application filter.

Version the posting. Preserve the version candidates saw, the date it was active, changes to requirements or pay disclosures, and any material clarification sent during selection. Otherwise, interviewers may evaluate applicants against a changed standard without realizing that earlier applicants received different information. Where the role changes materially, a documented decision is needed about reassessment or reopening, not a silent rewrite of the original criteria.

For candidates, the corresponding practice is a requirement-to-evidence map. Record the stated requirement, the best truthful supporting example, any gap, and the question needed to resolve ambiguity. This is not a license to manufacture an exact keyword match. It is a disciplined way to decide where the candidacy is strong, where transferable evidence may help, and where a genuine prerequisite is missing.

## 08. The candidate evidence inventory

Section-ID: HIR-008. Tags: career-evidence; provenance; master-record; privacy. Evidence type: original recommended method.

Maintain a private master record of employment, education, licenses, certifications, projects, contributions, and outcomes. The master is not the submitted résumé. It is a controlled evidence inventory from which appropriate documents can be assembled. Each material claim should have a basis: an employment record, a project artifact the candidate is permitted to use, a credential issuer, a contemporaneous note, or an explicit statement whose uncertainty remains visible.

For each role, record the employer's legal or public name, actual title, dates at the available precision, employment type, responsibilities, team context, and selected accomplishments. Distinguish an official title from an explanatory functional label. A truthful clarification might show the official title followed by a concise description of scope. Replacing it with a materially more senior title creates a different claim and should not be done merely to improve keyword alignment.

An accomplishment record should separate action, scope, result, and attribution. “Contributed to an integration program” is not equivalent to “delivered the enterprise transformation.” A team result can be relevant, but the candidate's own role must be clear. When a metric is estimated, label it and retain the estimation basis. When a result cannot be substantiated, use an accurate qualitative description rather than inventing a percentage.

Build a disclosure boundary alongside the evidence inventory. Mark information as public, approved for confidential interview discussion, restricted, or unknown. Employer and client materials do not become a candidate's publication property merely because the candidate worked on them. Use sanitized examples where authorized. A diagram reconstructed from memory can still expose sensitive architecture, customer identities, or strategy; anonymization must address substance, not only names.

Maintain version control at the document level even without Git. An application packet should record employer, requisition, submission date, résumé version, cover letter version, answers supplied, supporting files, and receipt. Preserve the exact submitted copy. A later improvement to the master should not silently replace what was actually sent. This enables useful interview preparation and a reliable record of factual consistency.

The master should not become a public knowledge file containing identity documents, Social Security numbers, reference contact details, medical information, salary records, or credentials. A public career-advice resource can use synthetic examples and general methods. Candidate-specific information belongs in a separately authorized private evidence store. This separation makes the same guidance safely reusable by different people and hosts.

## 09. Business résumés, academic CVs, and federal résumés

Section-ID: HIR-009. Tags: resume-types; CV; federal-resume; page-limits. Evidence type: guidance and recommendations.

Select the document type for the decision being made. A business résumé usually emphasizes relevant experience, capabilities, and accomplishments. An academic CV may need research, publications, presentations, teaching, service, grants, and professional appointments. A federal application must satisfy the announcement and current federal submission rules. Calling every long career document a CV, or assuming that every CV should be compressed into a business résumé, obscures these different purposes. (Harvard MCS n.d.)

For a business résumé, a reverse-chronological structure is a useful default because it makes recent roles and progression visible. A combination format can add a concise skills or project summary while retaining a clear chronology. A purely functional arrangement can be useful in some circumstances, but it should not conceal who employed the candidate, when the work occurred, or how claimed capabilities were demonstrated. These are editorial recommendations, not universal ATS requirements.

Length is an information-design decision constrained by the recipient's instructions. One or two pages may suit many business applications; an experienced candidate should not erase highly relevant evidence merely to comply with an unsupported one-page rule. Conversely, years of experience do not justify an indiscriminate archive of every task. Select the evidence needed for the target role and remove repetitive descriptions. The document should be long enough to establish fit and short enough to make that fit discoverable.

Federal guidance is a dated exception to generic résumé-length advice. OPM's 2025 applicant guidance states that USAJOBS would restrict résumés to two pages beginning September 27, 2025. The guidance also emphasizes relevant experience details, including employer, title, dates, hours worked per week, and series or grade where applicable. Applicants must still demonstrate qualifications and supply other documents required by the announcement. Do not assume that an older multi-page federal résumé remains acceptable, or that a two-page limit excuses omission of required evidence. (OPM 2025)

A résumé's top section should quickly identify the candidate and relevant professional proposition. A summary is useful when it interprets experience rather than listing generic virtues. “Experienced, motivated, results-oriented leader” provides little discriminating information. A concise description of the work the candidate can perform, the environments in which it was performed, and one supported outcome is stronger. Personal branding must not inflate the candidate's level, credentials, or authority.

Use a stable contact method, an appropriate geographic description, and relevant professional links. Full home addresses, government identification numbers, dates of birth, and unrelated personal information normally do not help a business evaluator assess capability. A public professional profile and a confidential application record serve different audiences. The résumé should not become a substitute for a secure onboarding form.

## 10. Résumé content architecture and claim quality

Section-ID: HIR-010. Tags: accomplishment-writing; resume-evidence; tailoring; chronology. Evidence type: original editorial method.

A strong résumé translates work into decision-relevant evidence. Start each experience entry with enough context to establish employer, role, location where useful, and dates. Then select accomplishments that demonstrate the capabilities needed in the target position. Responsibilities explain the setting; accomplishments explain what changed because of the candidate's contribution. Neither should be fabricated to make the document more persuasive.

An effective accomplishment pattern is action plus scope plus outcome, with attribution made clear. A synthetic example is: “Coordinated release planning across four teams and introduced a shared readiness checklist, reducing unresolved handoff issues from the recorded baseline.” The final phrase should become a number only when the underlying measure exists. Another valid example is qualitative: “Created an integration decision record that enabled security and operations reviewers to resolve competing requirements.” Lack of a numerical result is not permission to invent one.

Distinguish scale from success. A large budget, customer count, or system count describes context but does not prove delivery quality. A candidate can explain working within a complex environment without claiming responsibility for the entire environment. Similarly, exposure to a technology is not the same as implementing it, and implementation is not the same as sustained production ownership. Use verbs that match the evidence: evaluated, designed, configured, developed, operated, governed, supported, or contributed.

Tailoring should change emphasis, not facts. Reorder relevant accomplishments, clarify terminology, select appropriate projects, and explain transferable capabilities. Keep dates, employers, credentials, and the substance of achievements consistent across applications. Where a role uses a different vocabulary, include an accurate equivalent rather than merely copying its keywords. A candidate who supported customer operations can explain the relationship to customer success without pretending to have held a different job.

Skill lists should be supported by experience or other evidence. Distinguish current working capability, prior experience, formal training, and awareness where the distinction matters. Avoid proficiency bars or unexplained numeric ratings that imply a measurement process that never occurred. A short list of well-supported capabilities is more useful than an exhaustive collection of tools encountered once.

Review the résumé in three passes. First, verify factual accuracy against the master record. Second, check relevance against the requisition. Third, check readability and extraction. Have a reviewer ask what the candidate actually did, what evidence supports the outcome, and what remains unclear. The aim is not to produce a document that sounds universally impressive. It is to make a particular, truthful candidacy easy to evaluate.

## 11. What an applicant tracking system does and does not prove

Section-ID: HIR-011. Tags: ATS; parsing; screening; ranking; automation-myths. Evidence type: vendor evidence and analytical distinctions.

An applicant tracking system is best understood as a collection of recruiting functions rather than a single decision algorithm. An employer may use it to publish openings, receive files, create application records, search fields, coordinate interviewers, send messages, apply workflow rules, and retain decisions. Optional components may score, recommend, or rank candidates. The actual configuration matters more than the product category label.

Separate six mechanisms when diagnosing a problem: file acceptance, text extraction, field parsing, eligibility rules, search or ranking, and human review. A rejected upload can reflect a technical constraint. A parser can extract text but place it in the wrong field. A knockout question can route an otherwise readable application out of consideration. A recruiter can manually decide not to advance a candidate. These are different causes and require different remedies.

Greenhouse documents specific reasons for unsuccessful résumé parsing, including complex layouts, images, headers or footers, and file constraints. Its stated consequence is that fields may not be populated and information may need manual entry. This supports testing simple layouts and checking the resulting fields. It does not support a universal claim that ATS products automatically reject a fixed percentage of applicants because of formatting. Product-specific limits should not be generalized to all systems. (Greenhouse 2026)

There is no single “ATS score” that applies across employers. A commercial résumé-scanning service may compare a document with a job description using its own method. Its score is not proof of how a particular employer's configured system will behave. Candidates should treat such tools as optional editing aids, not as authoritative forecasts of interview probability. Employers should be equally cautious about assuming that a vendor's match score establishes job-related validity.

Relevant terminology helps retrieval when it accurately describes experience. Include the employer's terms and standard acronyms where appropriate, but do not use hidden text, white-on-white keyword lists, invented credentials, or copied job descriptions as substitutes for evidence. These practices can undermine trust even where a parser accepts them. An automated system's inability to detect a false claim does not make the claim acceptable.

For employers, procurement should require a functional inventory. Identify which components merely organize records, which make recommendations, which determine progression, and which use personal information beyond the submitted application. Record configuration ownership and change control. A system described as “human in the loop” still needs a clear account of what the human sees, what can be overridden, and whether that person has time and authority to make a meaningful review.

## 12. File formatting and submission reliability

Section-ID: HIR-012. Tags: ATS-format; PDF; DOCX; text-extraction; accessibility. Evidence type: recommended quality controls.

Follow the employer's specified file type and upload instructions. Where multiple formats are accepted, use a clean, text-based document and inspect the actual result. Neither PDF nor DOCX is universally superior in every application system. A PDF can preserve visual layout while still having a poor text layer; a DOCX can contain tables or floating elements that complicate extraction. The practical question is whether the recipient can retrieve the intended content accurately.

Use conventional section headings, a logical reading order, ordinary text for contact information, and consistent role and date formatting. A single-column layout is a conservative interoperability choice, not a law of résumé design. Avoid placing essential evidence only in an image, logo, text box, or decorative sidebar. A visually attractive document is not successful if the recipient cannot identify the candidate, chronology, or qualifications.

Perform a plain-text extraction check before submission. Copy or extract the text, then verify that the name, contact method, section order, employers, titles, dates, education, and skills remain legible and correctly associated. Check that ligatures, accented characters, bullets, and special punctuation have not become meaningless symbols. Extraction success is a useful technical test; it does not prove that an employer's parser will map every field correctly.

After upload, review all prefilled fields. Correct employer names, dates, qualifications, and answers rather than assuming that the attached résumé controls the database record. Where the portal requires manual entry, supply accurate information at the requested level of detail. “See résumé” can be inappropriate when the field is necessary for eligibility review. When the system fails, preserve the error, follow the employer's support or accommodation channel, and do not submit fabricated information merely to bypass a validation rule.

Use meaningful filenames without unnecessary sensitive information. A name, document purpose, role identifier, and version date can be sufficient. Keep the submitted packet and receipt in the candidate's private record. Where a deadline applies, distinguish the time a file was prepared from the time the application was successfully received. A saved draft or uploaded attachment is not proof of final submission.

Accessibility should be tested as part of the process, not added after a candidate reports failure. Employers can evaluate keyboard navigation, field labels, error messages, timeouts, captions, and alternative ways to complete an assessment. Candidates should use the designated accommodation process where needed. A technical workaround should not force disclosure of unrelated medical history or put an applicant at a disadvantage for requesting access.

## 13. Cover letters and statements of interest

Section-ID: HIR-013. Tags: cover-letter; motivation; career-transition; written-response. Evidence type: purpose guidance and original recommendations.

A cover letter is a selective narrative explaining interest and relevant fit. It should complement rather than duplicate the résumé. University career guidance continues to distinguish the letter's explanatory purpose from the résumé's concise record of qualifications. This supports using a letter when the application requests one or when a short narrative can clarify important context. It does not establish that every recruiter reads every optional letter. (Harvard MCS n.d.)

A practical structure contains three elements: the specific opportunity and reason for interest; one or two supported examples tied to the role; and a concise closing. A career change, unusual progression, geographic transition, or cross-industry transfer may benefit from explanation. The letter should make the relationship between evidence and role clearer, not apologize for every difference from a job description.

Specificity is more useful than flattery. Refer to a verified business need, product, mission, or operating context only when it is relevant and accurately understood. Do not invent familiarity with a company or claim admiration for a project that has not been checked. A statement of interest can be authentic without pretending to know the hiring manager's internal priorities.

Keep the letter proportionate to the request. A one-page letter is a reasonable business default unless instructions specify otherwise, but the exact word count is an editorial choice. Generic adjectives, repeated résumé bullets, and long personal narratives can obscure the contribution being offered. Address the actual employer and role, verify names, and remove template remnants before submission.

For an optional letter, use a decision rule rather than a universal slogan. Submit it when it adds meaningful information and can be prepared accurately. Omit it when the employer discourages it or when it would merely repeat the résumé without benefit. Where the employer requests a letter, follow the requirement. This report makes no unsupported claim that cover letters are obsolete or that a particular percentage of recruiters rejects candidates without them.

An application text box may perform a similar function but has different constraints. Answer the actual question and respect character limits. A response to “Why this role?” should not become a pasted full letter with a salutation and postal address. Keep a private copy of the final answer so that interview preparation reflects what was actually submitted.

## 14. Applications, eligibility questions, and attestations

Section-ID: HIR-014. Tags: job-application; knockout-questions; attestation; consent; application-record. Evidence type: original operating recommendations.

The application is a structured record of a candidacy for a specific opening. It may collect contact information, employment and education history, relevant eligibility answers, availability, and acknowledgments. The employer should be able to explain why each field is needed, who may see it, and when it is used. “The vendor included it” is not a sufficient business rationale for collecting sensitive information.

Eligibility questions should correspond to genuine conditions. Work authorization, sponsorship, professional licensing, travel, schedule, and location can be relevant, but wording matters. Candidates should answer the question asked rather than infer that a desired answer is necessary to remain competitive. When the wording is ambiguous, seek clarification through the available channel or state the relevant condition in an appropriate free-text field. Do not misstate a legal status, qualification, or willingness to relocate.

Differentiate required qualifications from preferences. A requirement that can lawfully exclude an applicant should have a defined basis and an accountable owner. Automated knockout rules should be versioned and tested with representative cases, including edge cases. A rule that rejects an applicant because a parser put the license in the wrong field is an information-quality failure, not evidence that the person is unqualified.

Attestations should be read before signing. A statement that the supplied information is accurate is different from permission to obtain a third-party consumer report, consent to recording, an arbitration agreement, or agreement to employment terms. These documents should not be treated as interchangeable merely because a portal presents them together. Where a notice has a legally required form or sequence, the portal must preserve that requirement rather than compress every acknowledgment into a single checkbox.

Candidates should retain the submission receipt, requisition identifier, material answers, and attached versions. A confirmation email establishes that something was received; it does not necessarily establish completeness, eligibility, advancement, or a guaranteed human review. A portal status such as “under consideration” may be administrative rather than a promise of an interview. Interpret statuses conservatively unless the employer explains their meaning.

Employers should distinguish withdrawal, duplicate submission, incomplete application, eligibility failure, assessment outcome, role cancellation, and selection of another candidate. These dispositions support different conclusions. Combining them into a generic rejection category can distort both candidate communications and analysis of the hiring funnel. The objective is an accurate record, not the appearance of a perfectly efficient process.

## 15. Portfolios, credentials, references, and supporting evidence

Section-ID: HIR-015. Tags: portfolio; credentials; references; transcripts; evidence-verification. Evidence type: original recommended practice.

A portfolio is useful when it demonstrates work relevant to the role and the candidate has permission to disclose it. A polished artifact alone does not establish authorship, individual contribution, production use, or business impact. Accompany each selected example with a concise account of the problem, constraints, contribution, outcome, and what remains confidential. Label prototypes and synthetic examples rather than presenting them as client deployments.

Credential evidence should identify the issuer, credential title, completion or award date, and current status where relevant. Coursework, a certificate of completion, a professional certification, a license, and a degree are not interchangeable. A candidate can describe training honestly without converting it into a credential the issuer did not award. Employers should verify essential qualifications through an appropriate source rather than relying only on a badge image.

Transcripts and professional licenses may be required in particular occupations or government processes. Request them at the stage and through the channel appropriate to the purpose. A résumé should generally state the relevant qualification, not expose an entire educational or identity record to every initial reviewer. Where a credential has an expiration, renewal, disciplinary, or jurisdictional dimension, the employer must determine what verification is needed for the actual work.

Reference lists should be shared with permission from the people named. Explain the role being pursued and provide enough context for an accurate reference, without scripting false claims. Keep contact details out of a public knowledge resource. Employers should use a consistent set of job-related questions, record the source and date, and distinguish verified facts from opinions or hearsay. The rules governing a third-party background report may differ from an informal reference call; do not assume one process covers both.

A useful evidence hierarchy distinguishes direct work samples, independently verifiable credentials, documented achievements, reference observations, and self-description. The hierarchy is not a universal ranking: a writing sample may be more informative than a credential for one role, while a legally required license is essential for another. The decision should follow the job analysis, not the prestige or visual sophistication of the evidence.

Avoid demanding unpaid deliverables that resemble commercially usable work without clearly defining the purpose, time expectation, ownership, and permitted use. A short hypothetical exercise can be enough to assess capability. If an exercise requires substantial labor or production value, review compensation and legal implications before proceeding. A candidate should not have to disclose a former employer's confidential work to demonstrate competence.

## 16. Interview architecture and structured evaluation

Section-ID: HIR-016. Tags: structured-interview; scorecard; behavioral-interview; panel; validity. Evidence type: research synthesis and recommended design.

An interview is an evidence-gathering procedure, not merely a conversation that produces an impression. Structured interviews use job-related competencies, consistent core questions, and a defined evaluation approach. OPM describes this method as a way to assess relevant competencies systematically. Structure does not require robotic delivery; it requires that comparable candidates receive a comparable opportunity to demonstrate the capabilities being evaluated. (OPM n.d.a)

Selection research also cautions against repeating older validity figures without checking the underlying methods. Sackett and colleagues revisited meta-analytic estimates, identified systematic overcorrection, and reported revised estimates for common selection procedures. Their findings support attention to structured interviews while challenging overconfident inherited rankings. They do not validate every interview simply because an employer calls it structured. (Sackett et al. 2022)

The recommended interview plan starts with a competency-to-question map. For each competency, define what satisfactory evidence would look like, which question or exercise elicits it, and how the answer will be scored. Behavioral questions ask about past actions; situational questions ask how the person would respond to a relevant scenario. Both need follow-up that clarifies evidence without giving favored candidates extra opportunities unavailable to others.

A scorecard should separate observed evidence from interpretation. “Described consulting three affected teams before changing the release plan” is evidence. “Collaborative” is an interpretation that needs a criterion. “I liked the candidate” is a reaction, not a job-related assessment. Use anchored ratings, allow an explicit insufficient-evidence state, and require notes sufficient to explain the rating. Do not force uncertainty into a neutral score that looks like a measured result.

Assign interviewers distinct evidence responsibilities. Repeating the same résumé walkthrough across five meetings can burden the candidate without adding useful information. A panel can consolidate observation, but it can also create social pressure and correlated judgments. Ask interviewers to record their initial evaluation before group discussion, then document any change in conclusion and its basis. This is a recommended control against unexamined consensus, not a guarantee of bias elimination.

Before closing an interview, allow candidate questions and explain the next step accurately. Do not promise a decision date that the process cannot support. Candidate evaluation and employer evaluation happen simultaneously: the person is also assessing the work, manager, expectations, and conditions. Clear information is part of a sound selection process, not a concession that weakens employer authority.

## 17. Candidate preparation and interview communication

Section-ID: HIR-017. Tags: interview-preparation; STAR; examples; follow-up; candidate-questions. Evidence type: original practical method.

Prepare a small evidence bank rather than memorize a script for every possible question. For each relevant competency, select an example with a clear situation, task, action, and result. STAR is a useful organizing mnemonic, not a scientific guarantee or required answer format. The candidate should be able to explain personal contribution, alternatives considered, constraints, tradeoffs, and lessons without overstating responsibility.

Start with the question actually asked. A concise answer can establish the main point, then provide an example and relevant detail. When the question concerns a capability the candidate has not directly exercised, distinguish transferable experience from the missing experience. A credible response can explain how an analogous problem was handled and what would need to be learned. Fabricating an exact match creates downstream risk in assessments, references, and employment.

Research the role using reliable employer materials and the actual posting. Prepare questions about expected outcomes, decision authority, team interfaces, resources, performance measures, and known constraints. Ask how success will be assessed during the first months, which problems are most important, and what has made the role difficult. These questions help test whether the opportunity aligns with the candidate's capabilities and expectations.

For virtual interviews, verify the meeting link, time zone, audio, camera where required, and a fallback contact method. Choose an environment that supports communication and protect confidential information visible on the screen. Technical problems should be reported promptly and factually. Employers should not treat ordinary connection problems or a home background as evidence of job capability unless a genuinely job-relevant requirement is being assessed through a fair process.

Clarify tool-use expectations for technical and written exercises. An employer may permit reference materials or AI assistance in one stage and prohibit it in another. Follow the stated rules; do not secretly delegate a personal assessment or misrepresent an assisted output as unaided work. A candidate can ask whether the exercise is intended to measure recall, problem-solving, communication, or realistic tool-supported performance.

A thank-you or follow-up message can reinforce interest and clarify a specific point. It should be concise, accurate, and free of pressure or invented urgency. This edition does not claim that such messages are universally required or that failure to send one causes a fixed rejection rate. Preserve important clarifications about role terms, process changes, or submitted evidence in the private application record.

## 18. Assessments, simulations, and work samples

Section-ID: HIR-018. Tags: work-sample; assessment; job-simulation; testing; accommodations. Evidence type: official guidance and recommended controls.

An assessment should answer a defined selection question. Work samples and simulations can ask a candidate to perform tasks resembling the job. OPM notes that their suitability depends in part on whether the assessed capability is expected on entry; a task may be inappropriate as a barrier when the employer plans to train the skill after hiring. This is a reason to connect the exercise to the job analysis, not simply choose the most difficult test available. (OPM n.d.b)

Different tests measure different constructs. A timed knowledge quiz, coding exercise, writing sample, personality questionnaire, physical task, and situational judgment test are not interchangeable indicators of overall talent. Employers should identify the intended construct, the scoring method, the evidence supporting its use, and the limitations of the result. A vendor's dashboard cannot substitute for that explanation.

Federal employment discrimination principles apply to selection procedures, including tests that may appear neutral. The EEOC's overview explains that a procedure can create unlawful exclusion and that job-relatedness and business necessity matter in appropriate circumstances. Vendor ownership of a tool does not remove the employer's responsibility for its use. This is a legal framework, not an assertion that every difference in group outcomes proves unlawful discrimination. (EEOC 2007)

The recommended assessment brief states purpose, expected duration, permitted tools, data handling, accessibility contact, scoring criteria, deliverable format, ownership, and next steps. Use fictional or approved data. Avoid exercises that require disclosure of personal medical information, former-employer secrets, or unnecessary identity documents. A candidate should understand whether recording, proctoring, or automated scoring occurs before participating.

Evaluate the result in context. A narrow exercise can reveal a particular capability without establishing every requirement of the job. A failed task may reflect insufficient skill, ambiguous instructions, technical failure, inaccessible delivery, or a mismatch between the assessment and the intended work. The employer should have a process for identifying these possibilities rather than assuming every score is error-free.

When AI tools are part of the job, consider separating unaided foundational capability from tool-supported performance. A realistic exercise may evaluate how the candidate checks an AI-produced answer, identifies unsupported claims, protects confidential data, and explains decisions. Such an exercise should make assistance rules explicit. The objective is to measure responsible work, not reward covert tool use or punish permitted assistance inconsistently.

## 19. Remote and hybrid hiring without category errors

Section-ID: HIR-019. Tags: remote-hiring; hybrid-work; location; accessibility; logistics. Evidence type: original operating recommendations.

Record interview format separately from employment location. “Remote interview,” “remote position,” “hybrid schedule,” “distributed team,” and “work from anywhere” are different propositions. A posting should explain the actual location requirements, expected office attendance, travel, time-zone overlap, and any restrictions on where the employee can work. Candidates should verify these conditions rather than infer them from the interview platform or an informal description.

Design remote selection for evidence quality. Provide clear invitations, accessible meeting instructions, a point of contact for technical problems, and a reasonable way to reschedule when the process fails. Interviewers should know whether a session is recorded, who can access the recording, and how long it is retained. Candidate agreement to a video meeting should not be treated as unlimited permission to analyze every visual or behavioral signal.

Asynchronous interviews can reduce scheduling constraints, but they also remove opportunities for immediate clarification. Employers should test question clarity, preparation time, response time, retry rules, accessibility, and the relevance of recorded performance to the job. Candidates should understand whether the recording will be reviewed by people, analyzed by software, or both. Do not assume that the platform's convenience establishes its predictive value.

Use location data deliberately. A candidate may live in one state, interview from another, and apply for a position associated with a third. The applicable rules can depend on more than a mailing address. Record the relevant facts and route legal questions to the appropriate reviewer. The report's selected state examples are not a substitute for that analysis.

A remote hiring process also needs a controlled handoff to verification and onboarding. Secure channels, identity checks appropriate to the stage, equipment logistics, and work-authorization procedures should be coordinated without turning early applications into identity-document collection exercises. A remote offer should make the employing entity, compensation basis, work location, conditions, and start arrangements understandable.

Do not equate remote work with reduced rigor or with unlimited candidate availability. A strong process can be both efficient and structured. Consolidate redundant meetings, give each stage a purpose, and publish realistic expectations. The operating target is sufficient job-relevant evidence with proportionate candidate burden, not the largest possible number of interviews.

## 20. Equal opportunity, disability inquiries, and accommodations

Section-ID: HIR-020. Tags: EEO; disability; ADA; accommodations; pre-offer; post-offer. Evidence type: legal overview and recommended controls.

Disability-related inquiries and medical examinations require stage-specific treatment. EEOC guidance distinguishes pre-offer restrictions from post-offer examination and inquiry rules. Before an offer, employers generally may not ask disability-related questions or require medical examinations. After a conditional offer, different rules apply, including consistency within the relevant job category and limits on how results are used. This summary does not resolve every exception or occupation-specific requirement. (EEOC 1995)

An employer can ask about ability to perform job functions without soliciting a diagnosis. The EEOC also describes limited circumstances in which accommodation-related questions may be appropriate, such as an obvious or voluntarily disclosed disability. That is not permission for routine medical-history collection from all applicants. Questions should be connected to participation in the process or performance of the work, with confidentiality handled appropriately. (EEOC n.d.a)

The recommended information architecture separates evaluation evidence from accommodation administration. An interviewer may need to know that extra time or captions will be provided without needing to know the underlying diagnosis. Access should follow purpose. A request for accommodation should not become a negative score for enthusiasm, resilience, or “fit.” Record the process adjustment and its implementation without unnecessarily circulating medical details.

Voluntary demographic questionnaires, disability self-identification, veteran information, and ordinary job qualifications are different record classes. Their permitted or required collection depends on the governing framework. The presence of a questionnaire in an ATS template does not prove that every field is mandatory for every employer or that interviewers should see the responses. Current federal contractor changes make inherited checklists particularly risky.

Design evaluation criteria around the work rather than social similarity. “Culture fit” is too vague when it means whether the interviewer would enjoy socializing with the applicant. Define observable behaviors relevant to collaboration, safety, customer service, or professional judgment. A candidate's mannerisms, accent, age cues, appearance, or background should not become unexplained proxies for capability.

For candidates, the practical distinction is between demonstrating ability and disclosing unnecessary personal information. Use the employer's designated accommodation channel where needed and retain important correspondence. This resource does not advise a person to conceal legally required information or to make a particular legal claim. Where an inquiry appears improper or access is denied, obtain advice from the relevant agency or qualified professional using the actual facts and jurisdiction.

## 21. Background checks, references, and fair-chance sequencing

Section-ID: HIR-021. Tags: FCRA; background-check; fair-chance; consumer-report; adverse-action. Evidence type: legal overview and proposed workflow.

When an employer obtains an employment background report from a consumer reporting agency, Fair Credit Reporting Act duties can apply. FTC and EEOC guidance describes a standalone written disclosure, written permission, and a sequence for adverse decisions. Before taking adverse action based on the report, the employer generally provides a copy of the report and the required summary of rights. A subsequent adverse-action notice has separate content requirements. These steps are not replaced by a general application attestation. (FTC and EEOC 2014)

The exact source and use of information matter. A third-party consumer report, an employment-verification response, a reference conversation, a professional-license lookup, and a public internet search are not automatically the same legal process. Employers should identify the provider, permissible purpose, required authorization, information used, and decision stage. A vendor's completion status does not itself establish that the employer followed all applicable obligations. (FTC n.d.a)

State and local fair-chance rules can constrain when criminal-history information is sought and how it is considered. California's Fair Chance Act provides one important example of conditional-offer sequencing and an assessment and notice process before certain adverse decisions. Coverage, exceptions, and response requirements must be checked for the actual employer and role. This is not a statement that every U.S. employer follows an identical national “ban the box” rule. (California CRD n.d.)

A recommended background-check workflow has separate states: authorized, requested, returned, under review, candidate correction or response pending, adjudicated, and communicated. Avoid labeling a report “failed” when it contains information that requires review or may be inaccurate. Keep the report, decision rationale, and communications access-controlled. Recruiters and managers should receive only the information necessary for their responsibilities.

Candidates should verify the legitimacy of the request, read the disclosure, use the secure provider channel, and review any report received. A discrepancy can reflect a record error, identity confusion, incomplete information, or a genuine issue. Respond through the appropriate process with accurate evidence. Do not assume that silence means a disputed record has been corrected or that an offer condition has been satisfied.

The distinction between decision support and decision responsibility remains essential. A background vendor can collect information, but the employer must determine what it may lawfully consider and how it affects the particular job. A standardized process should support consistent reasoning without creating blanket exclusions disconnected from the work or the applicable law.

## 22. Offers, conditions, and the boundary with onboarding

Section-ID: HIR-022. Tags: offer-letter; conditional-offer; verification; acceptance; onboarding. Evidence type: original operating model with official form references.

An offer is not the same as a completed hire. The offer record should state the employing entity, role, compensation terms, work arrangement, expected start date, conditions, acceptance process, and relevant deadlines. Material terms discussed orally should be reconciled with the written documents. A conditional offer should identify its conditions clearly enough that both parties can understand what remains unresolved.

Distinguish compensation components. Base salary or hourly pay, variable compensation, equity, benefits, signing payments, relocation support, and reimbursement are not interchangeable. A range in a posting is not automatically a promise of the maximum, and a target incentive is not necessarily guaranteed compensation. Candidates should obtain clarification of material terms rather than rely on shorthand such as “total package.” Employers should avoid communicating terms that conflict across recruiter messages and formal documents.

A recommended offer approval record identifies who approved the role, pay, exceptions, conditions, and start date. Keep negotiation records professional and factual. Do not place medical details or unrelated sensitive information in an approval summary. When an offer changes, preserve the prior version and explain the change rather than silently replacing the record.

After acceptance, onboarding can collect payroll, benefits, emergency-contact, equipment, policy-acknowledgment, and other appropriate records. Form W-4 concerns federal income-tax withholding; it is not evidence of job competence. Form W-9 supplies taxpayer identification for certain reporting purposes and does not, by itself, establish that a worker is correctly classified as an independent contractor. Worker classification requires a separate analysis under applicable law. (IRS n.d.a; IRS n.d.b)

Contracts, restrictive covenants, arbitration agreements, intellectual-property terms, confidentiality commitments, and repayment provisions may accompany offers or onboarding. Their enforceability and implications vary. This resource does not prescribe legal language for them or claim a universal national rule. Candidates and employers should review consequential terms with appropriate advice rather than treating every signature as routine paperwork.

A clean handoff identifies unresolved conditions and the owner of each next action. An accepted offer is not proof that a background check, license verification, work-authorization process, or start-date change has been completed. Conversely, completion of an onboarding form does not authorize the employer to ignore an unresolved contractual or legal condition. The record should describe the actual state.

## 23. Form I-9 and work-authorization documentation

Section-ID: HIR-023. Tags: I-9; work-authorization; E-Verify; document-choice; post-acceptance. Evidence type: official guidance and implementation cautions.

Work-authorization questions in an application and Form I-9 verification are different activities. Form I-9 belongs to the employment-verification process, not a general résumé attachment. USCIS guidance requires the employee's Section 1 to be completed no later than the first day of employment. The employer's Section 2 review generally must be completed within three business days after employment begins, with special timing rules for short employment. Consult the current instructions for the actual situation. (USCIS 2026; E-Verify 2026)

Document choice is an important boundary. The Department of Justice explains that employers may not demand particular documents based on a worker's citizenship status or national origin where the law gives the worker a choice of acceptable documents. A lawful permanent resident should not automatically be told to present a green card rather than another permitted combination. The employer's task is to follow the verification rules, not improvise nationality-based requirements. (DOJ 2025)

Remote hiring does not automatically authorize every employer to conduct a video-only document check. USCIS has procedures for an optional alternative examination process, and the employer must determine eligibility and comply with its conditions. The procedural guidance includes review of document copies and live video interaction, along with record-retention requirements. This edition does not reproduce a complete eligibility checklist; use current USCIS and E-Verify guidance before choosing the method. (USCIS n.d.)

The recommended systems design isolates identity and work-authorization records from ordinary interview materials. Do not put passport scans, identification numbers, or employment-authorization documents in a public portfolio or a broadly shared recruiter folder. Limit access to personnel with a legitimate process responsibility and use an approved secure transfer channel.

Candidates should distinguish a genuine onboarding request from a fraudulent recruiter attempting to collect identity documents. Verify the employer, role, sender, and provider through an independently established channel. An urgent message, familiar logo, or apparent offer letter is not sufficient proof. The FTC's job-scam guidance describes schemes that exploit job seekers through fake checks, fees, and requests for personal information. (FTC 2023)

## 24. Historical and specialized paperwork that should not become a universal checklist

Section-ID: HIR-024. Tags: form-inventory; WOTC; Form-8850; federal-forms; occupational-variation. Evidence type: current form status and scope controls.

A comprehensive resource should identify specialized records without implying that every applicant needs them. Government, education, healthcare, transportation, financial services, licensed trades, and security-sensitive roles can require different qualifications and verification. The actual announcement, law, contract, or regulator should determine the evidence request. An employer should not import a specialized form into an unrelated process merely because it appears in a generic hiring checklist.

One important historical correction concerns the Work Opportunity Tax Credit. The IRS states that Form 8850 is no longer in use and that the credit does not apply to employees who begin work after December 31, 2025. Earlier guidance describing Form 8850 as routine current pre-screening paperwork therefore needs an explicit historical label. This edition preserves the historical function while avoiding a recommendation to use the form for new 2026 starts. (IRS 2026)

Government applications may involve declarations, eligibility documentation, transcripts, veterans' preference evidence, suitability or security processes, or other announcement-specific materials. Do not treat these as interchangeable or presume that a general business résumé supplies them all. Equally, do not circulate a security questionnaire or highly sensitive supporting record in an ordinary recruiting packet. The request's authority and destination must be verified.

Occupational licenses, driving qualifications, professional privileges, safety training, and physical requirements should be tied to the job and applicable rules. A current credential may be necessary in one jurisdiction but insufficient in another. The employer should specify what must exist at application, at offer, or by the start date. Candidates should state pending qualifications accurately rather than present an intended future credential as already earned.

Some employers use conflict-of-interest, outside-activity, intellectual-property, or prior-obligation questionnaires. These should be scoped to legitimate concerns and handled with appropriate confidentiality. A candidate's broad acknowledgment does not resolve every potential conflict. Where an existing obligation may affect the proposed work, document the issue and obtain qualified review instead of inviting disclosure of protected material.

Maintain a form register with owner, purpose, audience, trigger stage, legal or business basis, current version, required signature, retention category, and replacement or retirement date. The Form 8850 example demonstrates why this register matters: a form can remain in an old portal long after its intended program has changed. A modern interface does not guarantee current content.

## 25. Pay transparency and salary-history controls

Section-ID: HIR-025. Tags: pay-transparency; salary-history; compensation-range; jurisdiction. Evidence type: selected current legal examples.

Pay transparency is not one uniform national requirement. Employers need to determine which rules apply to the position, organization, location, and posting. A useful control separates the legal minimum from a voluntary practice of providing clear compensation information. The same organization may choose a consistent disclosure policy while still maintaining a jurisdiction-specific compliance analysis.

California's official guidance describes salary-history restrictions and pay-scale disclosure obligations, including posting requirements for covered employers with at least fifteen employees. The agency also addresses positions that may be filled in California. The exact threshold, geographic application, and definition of the pay scale should be read in the current guidance rather than inferred from a national summary. (California DIR 2026)

New York State's official FAQ describes its pay transparency framework, including certain positions performed outside the state that report to a New York supervisor, office, or worksite. The scope is more nuanced than either “all remote jobs are covered” or “only an applicant's home state matters.” The employer should establish the relevant reporting and work-location facts before choosing the rule. (New York DOL n.d.)

The recommended compensation record distinguishes the approved range, the range communicated publicly, the intended hiring range where different and lawful, and the actual offer. Explain the basis of the range and any variable components. A range so broad that it conveys little useful information can create confusion even where a formal disclosure exists. Do not conflate annualized salary, hourly rates, and total compensation.

Candidates can discuss expectations without supplying prohibited or unnecessary salary history. A useful response identifies the compensation sought for the role and asks for clarification of the employer's range and components. This report does not prescribe an answer that misstates prior pay or guarantees a negotiation outcome. Negotiation should remain grounded in the role, relevant capability, and actual terms.

Review recruiter scripts, application fields, third-party advertisements, and interview questions together. Removing a salary-history field from the main portal is insufficient if an agency or interviewer continues to ask the same question in an applicable jurisdiction. Document the instructions given to recruiting partners and the process for correcting inconsistent postings.

## 26. Automated hiring tools and legal status as of November 5, 2026

Section-ID: HIR-026. Tags: AEDT; AI-hiring; bias-audit; legal-status; NYC; Colorado; Illinois. Evidence type: selected jurisdictional examples, not comprehensive legal advice.

New York City's Local Law 144 illustrates why legal definitions matter. DCWP's FAQ explains that the law covers specified automated employment decision tools used to substantially assist or replace discretionary decisions in covered hiring or promotion contexts. Its requirements include an independent bias audit within the preceding year, publication of audit information, and required notices. The FAQ distinguishes assessment of applicants for specific positions from certain sourcing activities. A generic ATS label does not resolve coverage. (NYC DCWP 2023)

The same FAQ makes an important limitation clear: an audit requirement is not a general certification that a tool is lawful or free of discrimination. Other antidiscrimination obligations remain relevant. Notice includes information about requesting reasonable accommodation under other laws and must follow the applicable timing rules. Do not convert a notice provision into a claim that every candidate has an unconditional right to any alternative process requested. (NYC DCWP 2023)

Illinois's original Artificial Intelligence Video Interview Act, effective January 1, 2020, required notice, explanation, and consent before specified AI analysis of video interviews for Illinois positions, and included sharing and deletion provisions. This historical act demonstrates that recording and analysis can trigger distinct obligations. It is not presented as a complete statement of Illinois law in 2026; subsequent amendments and other employment protections require current verification. (Illinois General Assembly 2019)

Colorado requires especially careful date handling. The Attorney General's current rulemaking page states that Senate Bill 26-189, signed in May 2026, repeals and reenacts the earlier 2024 provisions and that the new requirements take effect January 1, 2027. As of this edition, that is an enacted future-effective framework, not a rule already effective on November 5, 2026. The page also describes ongoing rulemaking. Reusing the original 2024 implementation timetable would be misleading. (Colorado Attorney General 2026)

The recommended control is a tool-and-jurisdiction register. Identify the function, decision influence, users, affected population, data inputs, vendor and model version, notices, assessment evidence, audit status, accessibility process, and human decision owner. Record enacted, proposed, effective, amended, repealed, and litigated status separately. A vendor release can change functionality even when the product name remains the same.

Do not infer honesty, emotional stability, personality, or suitability from facial expressions or vocal characteristics merely because software can output a score. A claim about a construct needs appropriate evidence, a job-related use, and lawful implementation. This edition does not endorse facial-emotion inference as an established hiring best practice or forecast its inevitable adoption.

## 27. Candidate data governance, retention, and security

Section-ID: HIR-027. Tags: privacy; retention; consent; records-management; access-control. Evidence type: official retention example and original governance recommendations.

A hiring record can contain both ordinary professional information and highly sensitive personal data. Govern the record by purpose and stage. The recommended data inventory identifies each field, why it is collected, whether it is necessary, who can access it, where it is stored, which providers receive it, and what event starts its retention period. Collection should not be expanded simply because storage is inexpensive.

Retention rules vary. EEOC's summary of selected obligations under 29 CFR Part 1602 distinguishes employer categories and record types; its general private-employer personnel-record rule is not a universal one-year deletion rule for every hiring record. Charge-related preservation can extend retention. Employers must reconcile applicable federal, state, contractual, litigation-hold, and business requirements before deletion. (EEOC n.d.b)

Separate active recruiting use from retained evidence. A record retained to satisfy a legal hold does not automatically justify continued marketing outreach or reuse in a new predictive model. Similarly, permission to apply for one position should not be treated as unlimited permission to distribute the applicant's data. The precise legal requirements vary, but the operational question should always be explicit: what use is authorized and necessary?

Access controls should reflect role. Interviewers need the evidence relevant to their evaluation, not payroll forms, identity scans, or medical details. Recruiting partners should receive only approved information. Candidate records should not be copied into public AI tools, unapproved note-taking services, or personal accounts merely for convenience. A confidentiality notice is not a substitute for secure systems and disciplined handling.

A recommended correction process allows inaccurate structured information to be identified and resolved while preserving an audit trail. Do not silently overwrite a submitted answer and then present the modified record as the original. Record who made the correction, why, the evidence supporting it, and whether the change affected a decision. The same principle applies to duplicate-record merges and data imported from external profiles.

Security training should include recruiting fraud. The FTC describes job scams involving payment demands, fake checks, and identity theft. Employers can reduce impersonation risk by clearly identifying official recruiting channels and explaining which information is requested at each stage. Candidates should independently verify unusual requests rather than rely on the apparent urgency or branding of a message. (FTC 2023)

This edition does not provide a comprehensive analysis of state privacy laws, biometric statutes, or recording-consent rules. Those are explicit refresh topics when a process collects sensitive recordings, uses automated inferences, or operates across jurisdictions. A generic privacy notice cannot be assumed to satisfy every applicable requirement.

## 28. Decision records, candidate experience, and useful metrics

Section-ID: HIR-028. Tags: disposition; hiring-metrics; candidate-experience; audit-trail; measurement. Evidence type: original proposed management framework.

A decision record should make it possible to understand why an application progressed, stopped, or remained pending. Record the opening, criteria version, evidence considered, evaluators, outcome, date, and relevant next action. Avoid vague personality judgments or unsupported allegations. The purpose is not to create an elaborate defense after the fact, but to make the actual reasoning and process state visible while the decision is being made.

Candidate experience should be measured at meaningful stages. Recommended measures include application completion, technical failure, time awaiting a response, scheduling delay, assessment burden, withdrawal, offer acceptance, and unresolved questions. These measures should not be combined into a single score without understanding what each represents. A fast rejection is not necessarily a fair decision; a long process may reflect a necessary verification or an avoidable bottleneck.

Define denominators before comparing rates. An application-completion rate might be completed applications divided by started applications, but the definition of a start must be stable. Interview-to-offer rate depends on whether “interview” means any screen, a substantive assessment, or a finalist meeting. Time-to-fill and time-to-hire often use different starting events. This resource recommends documenting those definitions rather than importing a benchmark that measures something else.

Distinguish people, applications, and requisitions. One person can apply to multiple openings, and one opening can produce several offers. Counting all three as the same unit can distort conversion, diversity, and workload analysis. Separate canceled openings, candidate withdrawals, incomplete records, and employer rejections. Missing demographic information is not a basis for inventing the applicant's identity.

Evaluate quality measures carefully. Later performance and retention reflect management, job design, onboarding, resources, and working conditions as well as selection. A correlation between an assessment score and retention does not prove that the tool caused better hiring. Avoid using historical “successful employee” profiles as an unquestioned template, especially when past opportunity or evaluation was unequal.

Communication should match actual states. Tell candidates when an opening is canceled, when a decision is delayed, and what the next step requires where the process permits. Do not send an automated “we carefully reviewed your qualifications” message when the only event was an incomplete upload. A more precise message can preserve credibility without revealing confidential deliberations or making promises the employer cannot fulfill.

## 29. Future outlook, 2027–2035: evidence before automation

Section-ID: HIR-029. Tags: forecast; skills-based-hiring; credentials; AI; uncertainty. Evidence type: conditional forecasts and recommended monitoring.

Forecast A: stronger demand for demonstrable, verifiable capability. Confidence: moderate. The rationale is that document claims are easier to generate and degree-removal policies require alternative evidence to become operational. The skills-based hiring research shows why announcements alone are insufficient. A plausible response is greater use of well-designed work samples, structured interviews, and credential checks. The forecast is not that degrees disappear, nor that every employer will adopt better assessments. (Sigelman, Fuller, and Martin 2024)

Monitor actual changes in job requirements, assessment design, and the qualifications of people hired, not only public announcements. Evidence that would weaken this forecast includes persistent reliance on unchanged proxies, abandonment of work samples because of cost or burden, or a mismatch between claimed skills-based policies and actual decisions. Candidates should prepare evidence of capability without assuming that a policy headline removes every barrier.

Forecast B: more portable credential verification. Confidence: moderate for technical availability, lower for universal employer adoption. W3C's Verifiable Credentials Data Model v2.0 provides an issuer-holder-verifier framework. Such standards can support machine-checkable credentials, but verifying integrity or issuer identity does not prove that a claim is true, current, or relevant to a job. The standard does not require every credential system to use blockchain. (W3C 2025)

The management implication is to evaluate issuer trust, status, revocation, identity matching, privacy, and interoperability. A digital badge is not automatically equivalent to a regulated license or a demonstrated capability. A candidate-controlled credential wallet may reduce repeated verification, but only if employers and issuers accept compatible processes. Universal decentralized hiring remains a speculative scenario rather than a baseline prediction.

Forecast C: more governance around consequential automation. Confidence: moderate to high for continued attention, uncertain for the exact national pattern. The selected New York City and Colorado examples show different mechanisms and timelines, not a settled nationwide regime. Employers should expect a continuing need to identify tool functions, data use, notices, and decision accountability. A single vendor audit is unlikely to answer every operational question. (NYC DCWP 2023; Colorado Attorney General 2026)

Forecast D: continued coexistence of documents and structured evidence. Confidence: moderate. A résumé provides a compact, portable narrative; structured forms support workflow; assessments and interviews generate additional evidence. Replacing one artifact does not eliminate the underlying functions. The practical design question is how to avoid redundant data entry and improve consistency, not whether one fashionable format will make every other artifact obsolete.

Forecast E: clearer rules for candidate use of AI. Confidence: moderate; implementation uncertain. Employers may distinguish assisted preparation, permitted workplace-tool use, and prohibited impersonation during assessments. Candidates should expect authenticity to be tested through discussion of choices, limitations, and work samples. This is an inference from the evidence problem, not a claim that a specific regulation or universal policy already exists.

## 30. Scenarios and a decision-focused watchlist

Section-ID: HIR-030. Tags: scenarios; renewal; leading-indicators; governance; forecast-validation. Evidence type: original scenario design.

The baseline scenario is incremental integration. Employers retain résumés and applications, improve parsing and scheduling, and add targeted assessment or verification tools. Candidates continue to maintain both a portable document and structured profiles. The primary risk is hidden complexity: more tools create more data movement and inconsistent criteria without necessarily improving decisions. The recommended response is a clear process owner and an evidence register for each consequential function.

A second scenario is evidence-first selection. Employers define a smaller set of role-specific capabilities, collect short work samples early, and use structured interviews to examine judgment and collaboration. This can reduce dependence on prestige signals, but it can also increase applicant burden or create inaccessible testing if poorly designed. The scenario should be evaluated through actual outcomes, candidate access, and assessment quality rather than enthusiasm for the label “skills-first.”

A third scenario is regulatory fragmentation. Different jurisdictions apply different notices, definitions, audit obligations, privacy rights, and effective dates. Employers respond with configurable workflows or conservative common policies. The risk is treating a common policy as proof of compliance everywhere. The recommended response is a jurisdiction register linked to the actual tool, position, and decision stage, with an accountable reviewer for changes.

A fourth scenario is authenticity pressure. Large volumes of polished AI-assisted applications make surface presentation less discriminating. Employers may place greater weight on live explanation, verified contribution, and job-representative evidence. The risk is overreacting with intrusive surveillance or unreliable deception detection. A better response is to define what must be performed personally, explain permitted tools, and assess the quality of reasoning and verification through observable work.

The watchlist should track triggers that would change practice: revised federal résumé instructions; changes to contractor regulation; new or amended pay-transparency rules; automated-decision implementation dates; changes to background-check and fair-chance processes; retired forms; credential interoperability; and actual evidence on assessment validity. Do not count a vendor announcement as a verified employer outcome.

NIST's AI Risk Management Framework Playbook offers a voluntary organizing model through Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage. It can help structure questions about a hiring tool's purpose, context, evaluation, and controls. It is not a legal safe harbor, a product certification, or proof that a system is fair. Use it as a governance aid alongside applicable law and job-specific evidence. (NIST 2023)

## 31. Reusable artifact specifications

Section-ID: HIR-031. Tags: templates; evidence-ledger; scorecard; application-packet; form-register. Evidence type: original illustrative designs, not official legal forms.

### 31.1 Candidate claim record

Record a claim identifier; the precise statement; dates and context; the candidate's contribution; supporting evidence and its location; quantitative definitions where used; confidence or uncertainty; disclosure permission; and approved wording. Keep private source records separate from the public-facing statement. A claim such as “led a transformation” should identify what leadership responsibility actually existed and which part of the transformation the candidate owned.

A synthetic record might state: Claim C-014; designed an exception-review workflow; contributed as the business analyst; pilot completed in a single department; outcome supported by an approved project summary; no organization-wide savings figure available. The resulting résumé sentence should not become “saved the company millions through enterprise automation.” The purpose of the record is to prevent persuasive editing from silently increasing the claim.

### 31.2 Application packet record

Record employer and employing entity where known; requisition identifier; job-posting capture date and version; work location; submission deadline and time zone; résumé and letter versions; required answers; supporting files; date submitted; receipt; and current status with evidence. Add a field for unresolved questions and a field for permitted follow-up. Store this privately because the packet may contain personal information and confidential correspondence.

Use separate states for prepared, uploaded, submitted, acknowledged, under review, interviewed, conditionally offered, accepted, withdrawn, rejected, and canceled. These are recommended management states, not a promise that an employer uses them. The record should retain the employer's original status wording alongside the candidate's interpretation.

### 31.3 Interview scorecard specification

Record competency; question or exercise; expected evidence; rating anchors; observed response; limitations or missing evidence; initial rating; evaluator; and final decision relevance. An anchor should describe observable performance rather than a personality label. For example, an adequate prioritization answer may identify competing objectives and a defensible tradeoff; a strong answer may also explain stakeholder consequences and how the decision would be tested.

A scorecard should permit “not assessed” and “insufficient evidence.” Those states are not equivalent to poor performance. Where different interviewers evaluate different competencies, aggregate only comparable information. Do not average unrelated ordinal ratings into a precise-looking number without explaining the method and its limitations.

### 31.4 Notice and form register

Record form name; business or legal purpose; jurisdiction; covered population; trigger stage; required content; source authority; owner; version; effective date; retirement date; signature or acknowledgment method; destination; access class; and retention rule. Keep a copy of the version actually presented. A link to a continuously changing webpage is not sufficient evidence of what a candidate saw on a particular date.

### 31.5 Selection decision record

Record the opening and criteria version, candidates considered at the relevant stage, evidence used, reasoned disposition, unresolved verification, decision owner, approval, and communication. Keep lawful recordkeeping and confidentiality requirements in view. The record should make clear whether a decision reflects qualification, comparative assessment, withdrawal, role cancellation, or another reason. It should not retrofit a reason after the outcome simply to make the file look complete.

## 32. Frequently retrieved questions

Section-ID: HIR-032. Tags: FAQ; ATS-myths; resume-length; cover-letter; paperwork-timing. Evidence type: concise synthesis with section cross-references.

### Does a parsing failure mean an ATS rejected the applicant?

No universal conclusion follows. Parsing, workflow rules, scoring, and human review are distinct functions. Greenhouse's documented unsuccessful-parse behavior is an example of fields requiring correction, not proof of automatic rejection. Check the actual platform and application state. See Sections 11 and 12. (Greenhouse 2026)

### Is a résumé always limited to one page?

No general rule in this report establishes that. Follow the recipient's instructions and choose relevant evidence. Business résumés, academic CVs, and federal submissions have different purposes and constraints. Current USAJOBS guidance introduced a two-page limit in 2025; a historical federal-resume convention should not override the announcement. See Section 09. (OPM 2025)

### Are cover letters obsolete?

This edition does not support that universal claim. Follow the request. Where optional, a letter is useful when it contributes relevant explanation rather than repetition. Its purpose differs from the résumé's evidence summary. See Section 13.

### Should an applicant submit identity and tax forms with the résumé?

Not as a generic application practice. Qualification evidence, work-authorization verification, payroll withholding, and contractor reporting have different purposes and stages. Use the employer's verified secure process and the current instructions for any required form. See Sections 22 through 24.

### Does a bias audit prove that a hiring tool is fair?

No. A specified audit is evidence about the audited scope and method, not a universal certification of validity, legality, accessibility, or fairness. Applicable requirements and the actual use still matter. See Section 26. (NYC DCWP 2023)

### Can a candidate use AI to prepare an application?

Preparation assistance should preserve truthfulness, confidentiality, and the employer's instructions. Do not invent achievements, impersonate the candidate in a prohibited assessment, or submit work under false claims of authorship. Distinguish editing support from an exercise intended to measure personal performance. See Sections 08, 17, and 18.

### Does removal of a degree requirement establish skills-based hiring?

Not by itself. The assessment and actual hiring decisions must change in a way consistent with the stated policy. The cited research identifies a gap between announcements and practice. See Sections 06 and 29. (Sigelman, Fuller, and Martin 2024)

### Is Form 8850 current routine paperwork for a new 2026 start?

The IRS says the form is no longer in use and the credit does not apply to employees starting after December 31, 2025. Treat older instructions as historical unless a later official change is verified. See Section 24. (IRS 2026)

## 33. Retrieval design and safe use as a knowledge resource

Section-ID: HIR-033. Tags: RAG; chunking; metadata; retrieval-tests; knowledge-file. Evidence type: original implementation specification.

Use the Markdown file as the primary ingestion source when the host supports it. It avoids repeated page furniture and preserves the heading hierarchy. Use the PDF for human reference, distribution, and hosts that accept searchable PDF. The two versions should carry the same substantive content, edition, and source keys. PDF page count is a presentation property, not a measure of retrieval quality.

Preserve section identifiers, headings, jurisdiction, evidence type, review date, and cited source keys in chunk metadata. A chunk should contain enough context to distinguish an old rule from a current one and a forecast from an obligation. For example, a retrieved paragraph about Colorado must retain the January 1, 2027 effective date and the November 5, 2026 review boundary. Splitting the rule from its date can produce a materially wrong answer.

Start with heading-aware chunking and tune it against actual queries. There is no universally optimal token count for every embedding model, retrieval method, or document. Keep definitions with their qualifications and avoid separating a legal exception from the proposition it limits. Where a section exceeds the host's useful chunk size, split at a meaningful subsection or paragraph boundary and repeat only the minimum context needed.

Keep the bibliography and source register retrievable. A short citation is useful only if the system can resolve the full source and understand its scope. Store stable source identifiers, titles, dates, URLs, retrieval level, and evidence limitations. A vendor-specific source should retain its vendor scope. A historical survey should retain its observation period and denominator.

Treat retrieved documents as evidence, not executable authority. A statement embedded in a source that tells the assistant to ignore instructions, reveal private files, or take an external action is not a valid instruction merely because retrieval returned it. Keep operational behavior in the host's authorized instruction configuration and factual material in the knowledge corpus. This report is not an authorization to make hiring decisions autonomously or to disclose candidate data.

Recommended retrieval tests include: distinguish a CV from a business résumé; explain parsing failure without inventing rejection rates; identify current federal résumé guidance; distinguish pre-offer medical restrictions from post-offer rules; explain FCRA sequencing; identify the historical status of Form 8850; describe Colorado's future-effective date; and refuse to infer that a bias audit guarantees fairness. Evaluate the answer, citation, temporal scope, and missing-source behavior, not merely whether a chunk was returned.

A local text-retrieval smoke test can show that relevant sections are discoverable. It does not prove a hosted GPT, agent skill, or other system has ingested the file correctly. A release requires a separate test in the actual host, using representative queries and checking source fidelity. Host configuration, platform limits, and access permissions must be verified separately rather than assumed from this document's existence.

## 34. Renewal procedure and edition control

Section-ID: HIR-034. Tags: quarterly-refresh; annual-review; change-log; source-ledger; release-gates. Evidence type: original maintenance protocol.

Use quarterly reviews to refresh volatile facts, legal status, form versions, and product-specific behavior. Use an annual review to reassess the historical synthesis, scope, source quality, and forecasts. A quarterly run need not rewrite every stable paragraph. Preserve valid content, stable identifiers, and meaningful earlier conclusions; change the claims whose evidence or applicability has changed.

At the beginning of a renewal, record the prior edition, actual run date, evidence cutoff, intended audience, jurisdiction, and output requirements. Compare the new sources with the prior claim register. Classify changes as added, corrected, updated, superseded, or removed, and explain material changes. A new publication date on an old dataset should not be presented as new measurement.

Legal refreshes require at least four separate dates where applicable: enactment, publication, effective date, and enforcement date. Also record amendments, rescissions, relevant court status when verified, and unresolved interpretation. If an official source is unavailable, retain the access limitation and narrow the claim. Do not replace an unread legal source with a confident paraphrase from a stale commercial summary.

Forecast review should compare prior expectations with observations. Record whether the predicted direction is supported, weakened, not yet testable, or abandoned. Preserve enough history to learn from the forecast rather than silently moving its deadline. A scenario can remain useful without being promoted into a prediction. Confidence should reflect evidence and uncertainty, not the writer's rhetorical strength.

Run two distinct quality passes before release. Pass A checks the files: actual PDF page count, selectable text, heading sequence, metadata, citations, bibliography correspondence, links, hashes, and visible layout. Pass B challenges the content against the user's requirements: scope drift, unsupported universal claims, stale legal status, conflated paperwork stages, privacy leakage, and claims of tests or deployment that did not occur. A second execution of the same script is not an independent review.

Keep the report, regeneration protocol, source register, change record, and validation evidence together. Preserve prior editions rather than overwrite them without recovery. Do not publish private candidate evidence as part of a generic knowledge-file update. The separate renewal megaprompt in this package supplies an executable authoring contract; it is not itself evidence that a quarterly task has been scheduled or that any host has been updated.

## 35. Compact glossary

Section-ID: HIR-035. Tags: glossary; definitions; retrieval-anchors. Evidence type: working definitions; legal definitions control where applicable.

**Applicant tracking system (ATS):** Software supporting recruitment records and workflows. Parsing, search, screening, ranking, and human evaluation are distinct functions.

**Application:** A submission associated with an opening, including structured answers and required supporting artifacts. It is not identical to the attached résumé.

**Assessment:** A procedure used to obtain evidence relevant to selection. Its intended construct, scoring, validity, accessibility, and lawful use require attention.

**Bias audit:** An evaluation defined by its scope, method, data, and governing requirement. It is not a universal certificate of fairness or legality.

**Candidate evidence inventory:** A private, source-aware record of career facts and achievements from which appropriate application materials can be prepared.

**Conditional offer:** An offer subject to stated requirements or verification. It is not proof that every condition has been satisfied.

**Curriculum vitae (CV):** A detailed professional or scholarly record, especially in U.S. academic and research contexts. The employer's request determines required content.

**Disposition:** The recorded outcome or state of an application, such as withdrawal, cancellation, nonselection, or advancement.

**Job analysis:** Examination of tasks and competencies used to support role definition and selection design.

**Knockout rule:** A configured eligibility or workflow rule that can prevent progression. Its existence does not establish its appropriateness or lawful application.

**Parsing:** Extraction and interpretation of document content into fields. Successful extraction is different from correct field mapping or a selection decision.

**Pre-adverse action:** A stage in applicable consumer-report decision procedures before a final adverse decision, with required information supplied to the affected person.

**Requisition:** The authorized organizational record of a hiring need, distinct from a public advertisement or offer.

**Résumé:** A selected account of qualifications, experience, and accomplishments intended to support evaluation for a role.

**RAG:** Retrieval-augmented generation, in which retrieved evidence is used to support generated answers. File availability is not proof of successful retrieval or faithful answers.

**Scorecard:** A structured record connecting evaluation criteria, observed evidence, and ratings or conclusions.

**Structured interview:** An interview designed around job-relevant competencies, consistent core questions, and a defined evaluation method.

**Work sample:** An exercise representing relevant job activity, interpreted within its scope and limitations.
