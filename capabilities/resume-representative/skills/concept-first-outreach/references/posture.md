# Posture decision

| Posture | Use when | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Apply-first | No real relationship with the decision maker, a clear fit on paper, or an imminent deadline | Fast and conventional; the résumé carries everything, and screening filters apply |
| Concept-first | A real, verifiable relationship and a concept that helps the organization, especially when the candidate's title history is a stretch for the role | Can lead to an invitation to apply or a consulting conversation; slower, and depends on the executive engaging |
| Hybrid | A relationship exists but a deadline or screening process makes waiting risky | Apply quietly, then lead the conversation with the concept; the application is disclosed if asked |

## Honesty rules

- Do not say "I'm applying" before an application exists. Do not deny one
  that does exist.
- Do not hide the candidate's interest when the executive asks. The prepared
  answer states it plainly and offers the deck and résumé.
- "Make them ask" describes the order of the conversation, not concealment.
  The concept must stand on its own merit to the organization.
- A signal of availability (for example, that a previous role ended) is fair
  when it is true and neutral.

## Fit signals for concept-first

- The candidate and executive worked together on something specific and
  nameable.
- The concept connects the candidate's experience to a threat or opportunity
  the organization faces, with public evidence.
- The candidate can explain the title gap without apology, through comparable
  leadership, scope, or proof of work.
