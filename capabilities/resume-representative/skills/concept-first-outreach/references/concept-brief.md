# Concept brief: the outsider's art of the possible

The brief shows how the candidate thinks about the organization's future. It
is written from outside, so it must earn trust through evidence and humility,
not through a plan the author cannot know is right.

## Structure

1. **The short version.** Five to eight lines a busy executive can read in a
   minute.
2. **A concrete scene.** One illustrative moment in the near future that makes
   the change tangible. Label it as an illustration.
3. **How we got here.** The industry's history in a few bands (for example,
   decade by decade), showing which channels were added and which ones stayed.
   New channels rarely close old ones; say so when it is true.
4. **The forces now.** Dated, cited market moves, competitor actions, and
   technology shifts. Separate fact from interpretation.
5. **What the organization could do.** Capabilities, each with a guardrail and
   the human role that remains.
6. **Scenarios, not predictions.** Two to four ways the next five years could
   look, with what would have to be true for each.
7. **The choice.** The decision in front of the organization, stated plainly.
8. **How it would find out.** Staged gates (for example: qualify, replay,
   shadow, limited live, scale) and the measures at each gate.
9. **Risks and what would change my mind.** The strongest counterarguments and
   the evidence that would prove the thesis wrong.
10. **Why this comes from me.** The shared history and relevant experience, in
    two or three sentences.
11. **Sources.**

## Rules

- **No first-30, 60, or 90-day commitments.** An outsider cannot know the
  organization's constraints. Offer questions and gates instead.
- **Label every estimate.** Any figure that is not sourced is a "working
  hypothesis" with its basis stated.
- **Cite primary sources** for company and market facts, with retrieval dates.
  When sources conflict, show both.
- **Respect the reader's position.** Do not tell the executive what their
  organization is doing wrong. Frame threats as external and choices as theirs.
- **Name competitors only with public evidence.**
- **Proof of work is allowed only when true.** A note on how the brief was
  produced (for example, with the tools it proposes) is fair if it is accurate
  and the candidate approved every claim.
- **Keep confidential detail out.** Nothing from a former employer's internal
  systems, plans, or data, even anonymized, unless the candidate confirms it
  is public.
