# Requirement-to-evidence mapping

This working record is private unless the candidate authorizes its audience.
Keep it separate from the employer-facing resume.

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Target | Employer, role, source and posting or requisition ID if available |
| Requirement | Actual requested outcome, activity, skill, tool, or constraint |
| Claim and source | Supported candidate statement and authorized source locator |
| Evidence state | Confirmed-from-source, candidate-reported, inferred, or unknown |
| Contribution and maturity | Individual/team role, dates, scale, POC/pilot/production where known |
| Result | Measured result with basis, qualitative outcome, or unknown |
| Disclosure | Permitted audience and any owner exclusion |
| Disposition | Include, qualify, ask, or omit, with reason |

Resolve each material claim independently. An owner-selected revised draft is
the working source, not blanket proof that every factual discrepancy is settled.
If a claimed metric, ownership, deployment stage, employment date, or restricted
project conflicts with evidence, retain the conflict and ask or omit it. Do not
trade privacy or accuracy for keyword coverage.

## Fictional worked example

A posting requests enterprise deployment experience. The candidate reports a
limited internal prototype, led its design, and does not know whether it was
broadly deployed. The working matrix records `candidate-reported`, `prototype`,
`design lead`, and `broad deployment unknown`. A defensible bullet can describe
designing the limited prototype; it cannot claim enterprise-wide production
rollout or attach an invented savings percentage. If the candidate excludes the
project, omit it entirely from the resume. Ask before asserting missing facts.
