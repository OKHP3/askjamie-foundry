### 1. Introduction & Brand Context

Mathews Archery is a privately held archery manufacturer known for pioneering compound bow technology and producing some of the most recognizable hunting and target bows on the market. Founder Matt McPherson, a mechanically minded bowhunter, invented single‑cam (SoloCam) technology in the early 1990s and launched Mathews Inc. in 1992. He set up operations in Austin, Minnesota and soon relocated the company to Sparta, Wisconsin, partnering with fellow archer Joel Maxfield and family members to build what would become one of the industry’s most influential brands[1]. The first single‑cam design used an idler wheel to simplify tuning, deliver true centershot and create a lighter, smoother and more forgiving compound bow[2]. By the spring of 1997 the company already offered six models and was experiencing rapid growth; between 1993 and 1997 sales reportedly grew by more than 2,600 percent[3].

Mathews remains family‑owned and purpose‑driven. McPherson has described the company’s mission as building quality bows “better than any other bow company” by sourcing the best materials and micromanaging every aspect of manufacturing[4]. The firm maintains its design, machining and assembly operations in Sparta, Wisconsin, where it employs more than 400 people and occupies a facility exceeding 56,000 square feet[5]. Unlike many competitors that outsource or rely on overseas production, Mathews’ bows are designed and built in the United States[6], a point the company highlights in its marketing and partnerships.

Beyond product performance, Mathews positions itself around faith‑based stewardship and community involvement. McPherson uses profits from his companies to support hundreds of missionaries and charitable organizations each year[7]. The company’s Lost Camo licensing division donates 100 percent of its profits to charities - primarily mission work - rather than taking a penny in revenue[8]. Mathews also created the affordable Mission Archery line to make quality bows accessible to families and youths, supports the National Archery in the Schools Program (NASP) through its Genesis bows and provides sponsorship to conservation groups such as the Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation (RMEF)[9]. These philanthropic commitments illustrate a corporate philosophy that pairs technological innovation with a “hidden agenda” of improving lives[9].

Quick identifiers:

- Founded: 1992 by Matt McPherson in Austin, Minnesota; headquarters in Sparta, Wisconsin[1].

- Ownership: Privately held, family‑controlled; employs over 400 people[5].

- Primary products: Flagship hunting bows, target/competition bows, Mission value‑line bows, Genesis youth bows, crossbows and a range of accessories (sights, stabilizers, quivers, rests).

### 5. Sustainability & ESG Commitments

Mathews does not publish comprehensive ESG reports, but several public‑source indicators reveal environmental stewardship and social responsibility:

- Charitable giving and mission support: Founder Matt McPherson uses profits from Mathews and related businesses to support over 700 missionaries and organizations such as Samaritan’s Purse and Prison Fellowship[7]. The company’s Lost Camo brand donates 100 % of its profits to missionary and charitable efforts[8].

- Youth & education programs: Through its Genesis line, Mathews supports NASP, providing the only bow approved for NASP competitions and promoting archery in schools[14][15]. Bowhunter notes that Mathews’ dedication to youth and affordable Mission products reflects a “hidden agenda” of improving lives and expanding archery participation[9].

- Conservation partnerships: Mathews became the primary bow partner of the Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation (RMEF) in 2016. RMEF credits Mathews’ support with helping conserve more than 6.8 million acres of habitat; both organizations promote ethical hunting and conservation[40].

- Manufacturing sustainability: A case study on Mathews’ machining facility reported installation of an automated chip‑processing and coolant‑recycling system that recovers aluminium chips and reuses coolant, reducing moisture content from 15 % to about 1 % and cutting waste and labour costs[5]. Maintaining all machining and assembly operations in Sparta allows tighter control over waste and emissions[4].

- Local economic impact: By designing and building bows in the USA and sourcing materials domestically, Mathews supports local jobs and reduces shipping emissions compared with companies that outsource manufacturing[6].
