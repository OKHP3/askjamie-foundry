### 1. Introduction & Brand Context

Mathews Archery is a privately held American archery manufacturer known for pioneering single‑cam compound bow technology and for driving the broader bowhunting and target‑archery markets toward high‑performance yet shooter‑friendly equipment. The company traces its roots to inventor and bowhunter Matt McPherson, who built his first compound bow at age 13 in the early 1970s and later patented an “Inner Cam” design[1]. McPherson founded McPherson Archery in 1985 to commercialize that technology but sold his interest in 1989[1]. Unsatisfied with the bureaucratic constraints of a partnership structure, he began a new venture: Mathews Inc., established in 1992[2]. The fledgling firm operated initially out of a 3,000‑sq‑ft steel building in Austin, Minnesota and employed just two people[3]. Within three years its innovative single‑cam “SoloCam” bows were in high demand, and by 1997 the company had released six models and ranked on Inc. magazine’s list of America’s fastest‑growing companies with a sales increase of 2,666 percent. Today the company is still owned by Matt and Sherry McPherson[4] and is headquartered in Sparta, Wisconsin, where it operates a manufacturing campus exceeding 180,000 sq ft and employs hundreds of people[5]. Mathews’ mission is guided by the core values Innovation, Integrity and Impact[6]. Beyond manufacturing, the firm supports conservation efforts, invests in archery education and promotes a family‑oriented work culture[7].

### 4. Market Position / Competitive Dynamics

#### 4.1 Market overview

The global archery equipment market was valued at approximately US$3.34 billion in 2024 and is projected to reach US$4.63 billion by 2032[15]. Compound bows represent nearly 48 % of market revenue[16], and North America accounts for roughly 36–41 % of sales[15]. Mathews competes primarily in the premium hunting and target‑compound segment, where technology differentiation and brand loyalty allow for higher margins. The company sells exclusively through authorized dealers and consciously avoids mass‑market retailers[10]. According to a 2023 Hunting Retailer survey of 150 pro‑shop owners, Mathews bows were stocked by 74 % of shops - higher than rivals Hoyt (65 %), Bowtech (63 %), Elite (52 %), PSE (51 %) and Bear (42 %)[17]. The same survey noted that Mission crossbows were carried by 44 % of retailers, underscoring the brand’s strength in the crossbow category[17].

### 5. Sustainability & ESG Commitments

Mathews’ social responsibility framework flows from the “Impact” pillar of its values. Matt McPherson and his team explicitly link business success with positive community outcomes, particularly in youth education, conservation and humanitarian outreach. The company does not publish a formal environmental, social and governance (ESG) report, but publicly available information highlights several commitments:

- Archery education: Mathews co‑founded the National Archery in the Schools Program (NASP) in 2002 by partnering with the Kentucky Department of Fish & Wildlife Resources and the Department of Education[11]. McPherson personally provided seed funding to launch the program, and the company continues to donate bows and financial resources. By 2024 NASP engaged 1.3 million students annually across more than 9,500 schools[11], and the Genesis bow remains its official equipment[6].

- Conservation and hunting heritage: Mathews is a corporate partner of the Pope & Young Club, which preserves bowhunting records and advocates for ethical hunting[13]. Vice President Joel Maxfield has stated that preserving bowhunting tradition is vital for the brand[13]. The company also supports Sportsmen’s Alliance at the President’s Club level, recognizing the need to defend hunting rights[14].

- Philanthropy & missionary support: McPherson and his wife operate mission outreach programs; profits from the Lost Camo camouflage licensing are donated to mission work in Africa, including building wells, churches and schools[12]. Company blog posts describe employees travelling to Atlanta suburbs annually to harvest surplus deer and donate processed venison to food banks, as well as local blood drives, food drives and donations to the Boy Scouts and Boys & Girls Clubs[18].

- Employee & customer welfare: Mathews emphasizes a “people before business” philosophy. Authorized Mathews retailers are carefully selected for knowledge and service orientation[10]; inside the organization, the culture of respect and accountability contributes to low turnover and high job satisfaction[19]. All bows are covered by a limited lifetime warranty[20], and the company invests in training technicians to support customers.

### 7. Industry Trends / Partnerships / Collaborations

#### 7.1 Industry trends

Several macro trends influence the archery and bowhunting industry:

- Growing participation through education programs: NASP and similar initiatives continue to introduce millions of young people to archery. Mathews’ early investment in the Genesis bow and NASP sponsorship positions it to benefit from this pipeline of new archers[11].

- Technological arms race: Manufacturers compete on speed, vibration reduction and modularity. Lightweight, more stable risers and integrated accessories (e.g., Bridge‑Lock) are increasingly important. Crossbow technology is converging with vertical bows in terms of performance and safety.

- Regulatory & ethical considerations: Some jurisdictions debate crossbow usage in archery seasons. Conservation organizations emphasise ethical hunting and sustainability, pushing companies to align marketing with responsible practices. Partnerships with conservation bodies (e.g., Pope & Young) reflect this trend[13].

- Digital integration: Electronic sights, range‑finding devices and smartphone apps are being adopted. Mathews introduced a smartphone‑compatible Sensor accessory and invests in digital tools to attract younger consumers.

- Fragmented distribution landscape: While big‑box retailers drive volume, high‑end archers often prefer specialized shops. Mathews’ dealer exclusivity strategy remains a differentiator but may limit exposure in emerging markets.
