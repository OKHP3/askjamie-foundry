### 1. Introduction & Brand Context

Mathews Archery is a privately held American manufacturer of high‑performance compound bows and accessories. Founded in 1992 by engineer and bowhunter Matt McPherson, the company pioneered the single‑cam (Solocam®) bow and has remained a leading innovator in the archery industry[1]. Headquartered in Sparta, Wisconsin, Mathews designs and manufactures its products in the United States using high‑quality materials and skilled craftsmanship; its Limited Lifetime Warranty explicitly notes that all Mathews bows are built in the U.S. and constructed to last a lifetime[2].

From its earliest days the brand has been guided by three core values  -  Innovation, Integrity and Impact[1]. Innovation is reflected in continuous product advancements, integrity in the company’s people‑first culture, and impact in philanthropic commitments. The founder’s faith and personal mission have heavily influenced the company’s ethos; McPherson has stated that his foundation is not business or even archery but his relationship with Christ, which informs a broader calling to serve and give back[3].

Mathews positions itself as “a family of bowhunters, inventors, producers and storytellers”[4]. The company believes archery is an escape from modern life’s rigors and strives to elevate every aspect of the archery experience through products, service and storytelling[5]. A people‑first approach is evident in McPherson’s declaration that at Mathews, “People first, business second”[1].

Mathews matters in its sector because it dominates high‑performance compound bow markets; its innovations (e.g., Solocam, Grid Lock riser, Crosscentric cam) have influenced industry standards and shaped the expectations of both hunters and competitive archers. In addition to product leadership, the brand’s partnerships with conservation groups and youth archery programs (discussed below) position it as a steward of hunting heritage and outdoor access. Its privately held structure, with McPherson retaining sole ownership, enables long‑term commitment to innovation and mission rather than short‑term shareholder pressures.

### 5. Sustainability & ESG Commitments

#### Overview

Mathews does not publish a traditional ESG or sustainability report. However, public information reveals a company ethos built around long‑lasting products, local manufacturing, conservation partnerships and philanthropy. Its commitments span environmental stewardship, social impact and governance values. This section synthesizes documented initiatives and partnerships to provide a holistic view of Mathews’ ESG footprint.

#### Environmental stewardship

- High‑quality, durable products manufactured in the USA. Mathews explicitly states that its bows are manufactured in the United States with high‑quality materials by expert craftsmen and built to last a lifetime[2]. The Limited Lifetime Warranty reinforces this commitment, requiring registration and covering defects in materials and workmanship[6]. Durable products reduce the environmental impact associated with frequent replacements.

- Membership in World Archery’s sustainability programme. World Archery (the sport’s global federation) has committed to environmental, social and economic sustainability; it joined the UN Sport for Climate Action initiative and pledged to reduce carbon emissions by at least 50% by 2030 (compared to 2019 levels) and to achieve carbon neutrality by 2040, offsetting unavoidable emissions since 2020[15]. World Archery lists Mathews among the companies that have joined its sustainability commitment[16]. While Mathews has not publicly announced its own carbon targets, affiliation with this initiative signals support for industry‑wide climate goals and best‑practice sharing.

- Product excise taxes funding conservation. Under the U.S. Pittman–Robertson Federal Aid in Wildlife Restoration Act, manufacturers of archery equipment (including Mathews) pay a federal excise tax on each bow sold. These funds are distributed to states for wildlife management and conservation. The U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service notes that the National Archery in the Schools Program (NASP) was created in partnership with the Kentucky Department of Fish & Wildlife, the Kentucky Department of Education and Mathews; funds generated from the federal excise tax on firearms and archery equipment finance conservation projects and wildlife management[17].

- Conservation and hunting heritage partners. Mathews publicly lists a broad array of conservation partners on its “Who We Are” page. It states, “We are proud to partner with these organizations to ensure the future of conservation and bowhunting,” and features logos for the Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation (RMEF), Boone and Crockett Club, Whitetails Unlimited, National Wild Turkey Federation (NWTF), Wild Sheep Foundation, National Archery in the Schools Program (NASP), Quality Deer Management Association (QDMA), and the Sportsmen’s Alliance[18][19]. Such partnerships underline a commitment to habitat preservation, ethical hunting and wildlife management.

- Primary bow partner for RMEF. In 2016 the Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation (RMEF) announced Mathews as its primary bow partner. RMEF noted that Mathews produces “highest quality products” and that the company’s philosophy aligns with RMEF’s conservation mission[20]. RMEF has permanently protected or conserved more than 6.8 million acres of elk habitat[21]. By supporting RMEF, Mathews indirectly contributes to habitat conservation and public access for hunters.

- Sportsmen’s Alliance President’s Club member. Mathews joined the Sportsmen’s Alliance Business Partner program at the President’s Club level, a top tier of support. Mathews’ Vice President of Sales and Marketing Brad Treu said the company’s hunting heritage is important and that they are proud to support the Alliance’s efforts to protect hunting rights[22]. The Sportsmen’s Alliance advocates for legislative and legal protections of wildlife conservation programs and hunting, fishing and trapping traditions[23].

- Commitment to water and humanitarian projects. The company’s “Making an Impact” article reveals philanthropic work beyond conservation. It states that McPherson has dug wells to provide clean water to villages and supported missionaries around the globe[24]. While not quantified, these projects demonstrate social responsibility in vulnerable communities.

- Faith‑based storytelling and film projects. Through the Salvation Poem Project, McPherson’s family produced the animated film “Light of the World” to share Christian messages[25]. The initiative emphasises using art and storytelling to spread values of compassion and hope.

#### Social impact & community

- Youth education via NASP. In 2002 Mathews partnered with the Kentucky Department of Fish & Wildlife and the Kentucky Department of Education to create the National Archery in the Schools Program (NASP). The U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service notes that this program is funded by the federal excise tax on archery equipment and has introduced millions of students to archery[17]. Surveys reported by Archery Wire indicate that NASP participants are more likely to buy a bow, participate in archery or hunting, and spend more time outdoors[26][27]. The program fosters a generation of youth connected to the outdoors and conservation.

- People‑first culture. Interviews with company executives emphasize a “people before business” ethos. Brad Christian, a Mathews representative, described a family‑like culture where relationships matter and where employees are valued[28]. This culture encourages employee retention and well‑being, although specific workforce statistics are not publicly available.

- Community storytelling and engagement. Mathews invites customers to share their archery stories and adventures through its media platforms. The Who We Are page states that the company listens to its community and uses its platform to tell their stories, aiming to build community and inspire adventures around the globe[9].

- Service and warranty commitments. Mathews stands behind its products with a lifetime warranty. The FAQ page states that the company “stand[s] behind every bow we make” and encourages owners to register for the Limited Lifetime Warranty[6]. Warranty claims are processed through a network of independent retailers, ensuring personalized service and reinforcing the local dealer ecosystem.

#### Governance and ethics

- Privately held governance. Mathews remains privately owned by Matt McPherson. This structure allows quick decision‑making and enables the company to prioritize long‑term mission over quarterly performance[14]. However, it also means there is less public transparency compared with publicly traded companies; there are no published financial statements or formal ESG reports.

- Integrity as a core value. Mathews lists Integrity as one of its three guiding principles and emphasizes ethical treatment of customers, retailers and employees[1]. The founder’s faith and emphasis on “people first, business second” suggest a values‑based governance approach.

- Partnerships with ethical organizations. By aligning with RMEF, Boone and Crockett Club and other conservation groups, Mathews supports organizations known for fair‑chase ethics and scientific wildlife management.

#### Industry trends

- Sustainability expectations rising in outdoor equipment. Consumers increasingly expect brands to disclose environmental impacts and support sustainable practices. Mathews’ alignment with World Archery’s climate initiative and its durable products resonate with this trend, though the absence of a formal ESG report is a gap compared with some industries.

- Increasing youth participation through NASP. NASP continues to grow, with tens of millions of students introduced to archery[8]. As participation rises, there is heightened focus on safety, inclusivity and conservation education.

- Technological arms race. Bow manufacturers compete through incremental innovation (lighter weights, higher speeds, improved dampening). Mathews remains at the forefront but faces strong competition from Bowtech, Hoyt, Prime and others.

- Regulatory pressures on hunting and gun laws. Organizations like the Sportsmen’s Alliance highlight ongoing legislative battles over hunting rights; such regulation could affect demand for archery equipment and may require continued advocacy[31].

- Shift toward digital content and community. Brands increasingly engage audiences via social media, YouTube and streaming films. Mathews leverages storytelling platforms and films (e.g., Light of the World) to reach audiences beyond traditional advertising[25].

### 8. Challenges & Documented Future Outlook

#### Challenges & risks

- Lack of formal ESG reporting. Mathews does not issue ESG or sustainability reports, making it difficult to quantify emissions, supply‑chain impacts or workforce diversity. As consumer and regulatory demands for transparency increase, this absence could become a liability.

- Regulatory and social pressures on hunting. Anti‑hunting movements and changing wildlife management laws could reduce demand for hunting equipment. Mathews mitigates this risk through advocacy partnerships (Sportsmen’s Alliance) but faces an uncertain regulatory landscape[31].

- Supply‑chain volatility. Manufacturing in the U.S. offers quality control but may expose the company to domestic labor shortages or material price fluctuations. There is no public information on Mathews’ supplier standards or sourcing practices.

- Competitor innovation. Bow manufacturers continue to innovate; failure to keep pace could erode Mathews’ technical advantage. However, the company has a track record of regular breakthroughs.

#### Documented future outlook (non‑speculative)

- Continued innovation. USA Archery’s 2025 partnership announcement quotes Mathews representatives saying the company remains committed to improving bow performance and elevating the sport of archery[33]. This suggests ongoing R&D investment.

- Expansion of NASP and youth programs. NASP continues to introduce millions of students to archery[26]. Mathews’ design of the Genesis bow and continued support of NASP indicate a sustained focus on youth engagement.

- Stewardship partnerships. The long list of conservation partners on the Who We Are page implies ongoing collaboration. No end dates are specified, and the listing is actively maintained[18].

- World Archery climate commitments. World Archery’s 2030 and 2040 climate pledges[15] provide a framework that may influence Mathews’ operations and event participation. Participation in this initiative suggests the company will consider carbon reductions and offsetting.
