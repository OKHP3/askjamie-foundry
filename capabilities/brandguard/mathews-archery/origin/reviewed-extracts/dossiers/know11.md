### 8. Challenges & Documented Future Outlook

#### 8.1 Legal and regulatory risks

Price‑fixing and Minimum Advertised Pricing (MAP) litigation. In May 2025 a class‑action lawsuit filed in federal court alleged that the Archery Trade Association (ATA), retailers such as Bass Pro Shops, Cabela’s and Dick’s Sporting Goods, and manufacturers including Mathews, Hoyt, Bowtech and PSE conspired to fix retail prices for archery equipment by coordinating and enforcing minimum advertised pricing policies[14]. Plaintiffs claim that MAP policies, when enforced collectively, set artificially high prices and discourage retailers from discounting. SGB Media summarises court filings stating that the alleged cartel viewed price competition as an “existential threat,” and that the ATA encouraged members to jointly enforce MAP[28]. The lawsuit seeks damages and injunctive relief for hundreds of thousands of consumers[29]. Defendants have not yet responded. Outcome uncertainty and possible regulatory changes could force Mathews to alter its pricing strategy and dealer relationships.

Wildlife conservation and hunting regulations. Broader hunting regulations (bag limits, season lengths, mandatory hunter‑education programs) influence equipment demand. The pandemic temporarily disrupted bowhunter education participation (declining from over 160,000 students in 2019 to 121,295 in 2020)[31]. Participation is recovering but underscores how external events can affect the pipeline of new archers.

#### 8.2 Economic and supply‑chain challenges

Macro‑economic volatility and inflation. Mathews’ credit risk profile shows that the company’s probability of default spiked during 2022–23 as supply‑chain disruptions, inflation and rising borrowing costs increased operating expenses. By late 2025 the probability of default improved but remained sensitive to macro‑economic tightening. Inflation also affects consumers’ discretionary spending; high‑priced flagship bows may face demand elasticity.

Supply‑chain and labour shortages. Post‑pandemic manufacturing delays, raw‑material shortages and shipping bottlenecks have challenged archery manufacturers. Retailers interviewed by Archery Business reported persistent staffing shortages, requiring higher wages to retain skilled employees[9]. Inventory management is also difficult; some shops over‑ordered during pandemic shortages, leaving them with excess stock when demand slowed[7]. These issues increase working‑capital requirements and could pressure Mathews’ dealers and cash flows.

#### 8.3 Competitive and market headwinds

Intensifying competition and shorter product cycles. The presence of multiple high‑quality brands (Hoyt, Bowtech, PSE, Bear) means innovation cycles shorten and product differentiation erodes. Consumer expectations for lighter, faster and more customizable bows require continuous R&D investment[26]. If Mathews fails to deliver perceptible improvements, dealers may struggle to sell new models[7].

Consumer behaviour changes and demographics. After a pandemic‑driven boom, many customers feel they have purchased enough equipment, leading to a “pandemic hangover” and slower sales[24]. At the same time, consumers demand technical knowledge and customization, influenced by online content[20]. The overall decline in hunting participation (−1 % between 2011–2021), contrasted with a 6 % increase in bowhunting[22], suggests a need to attract new demographics such as women and youth, who are participating in growing numbers[32]. Mathews must balance high‑performance product development with affordability and accessibility.

Potential counterfeit and intellectual‑property risks. Market research firms warn that counterfeit and “knock‑off” archery products are proliferating, eroding brand equity and safety. While not specific to Mathews, the presence of counterfeit goods may undermine consumer trust and drive price competition.

#### 8.4 Documented future outlook

Despite these challenges, credible sources anticipate continued growth in the archery equipment market. Strategic Market Research predicts the global market will reach approximately USD 2.35 billion in 2025 and grow at a 5.28 % compound annual rate through 2030[3]. Demand is supported by rising participation in bowhunting and target archery, expanding youth programs like NASP and the popularity of crossbow seasons[33]. Mathews’ renewed partnership with USA Archery through 2025 and its major investments in the Lift series indicate a commitment to capture this growth[13]. However, long‑term prospects depend on the outcome of antitrust litigation, the company’s ability to manage supply‑chain volatility, adapt to demographic shifts, and maintain innovative leadership.
