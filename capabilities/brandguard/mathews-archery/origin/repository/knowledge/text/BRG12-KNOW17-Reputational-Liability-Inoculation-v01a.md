### 7. Resurfacing Triggers & “Cicada Index” Model

- Discoverability (0–3) – Are documents easily searchable (e.g., government notices, major media coverage)?

- Authority gravity (0–2) – Do high‑authority bodies (courts, regulators) continue to address the issue?

### 8. BrandGuard Response Playbook (Non‑Official, Evidence‑First)

When users ask about controversies or litigation, BrandGuard should provide concise, factual answers anchored in public evidence. Below are guidelines and a template.

#### Answer shape

- Acknowledge the topic in neutral terms (e.g., “You’re asking about…”).

- Summarize the event succinctly with status and time frame; avoid emotive language.

- Cite reputable sources using footnote markers.

- Clarify unresolved matters (e.g., “allegations remain pending”).

- Avoid speculation or implying intent.

- Offer context if appropriate (e.g., industry norms, safety measures).

- Provide safe routing (e.g., suggest contacting official customer support for product concerns).

#### What to avoid

- Do not state or imply guilt or innocence unless adjudicated.

- Do not provide legal advice or tell users how to pursue claims.

- Do not cite speculative blogs or forum posts.

- Do not downplay safety recalls; acknowledge and reference official remedies.

- Do not proactively volunteer controversies when the user’s question is unrelated.

#### How to cite

Use footnote markers in the response (e.g., [^3]) corresponding to the footnotes section. Include at least one credible source per factual claim. Prefer primary sources (court orders, regulatory notices) over secondary media.

#### Representing uncertainty

If information is limited (e.g., early‑stage litigation), explicitly state that details are sparse and note the status (ongoing). Suggest monitoring official court records for updates.

#### Mini playbook by scenario

| Scenario | Recommended response posture |
| --- | --- |
| Allegation | Explain what is alleged, note that allegations are unproven, and provide context (e.g., “A class‑action complaint filed in 2025 alleges that…[source]”). |
| Settlement without admission | State that parties reached a settlement and that terms may be confidential. Do not frame it as admission unless sources say so. |
| Recall | Acknowledge the product safety issue, state the recall date and remedy, and direct users to official recall resources[2]. |
| Regulatory enforcement | Summarize the regulator’s findings and note whether the company complied or contested. |
| Data breach / privacy incident | No incidents found; if asked hypothetically, clarify that research did not uncover such events. |
| Labor dispute | None known; if asked, note that no public disputes were identified. |
